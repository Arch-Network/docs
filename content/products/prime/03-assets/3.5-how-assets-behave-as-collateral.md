title: How assets behave as collateral

This page connects the assets in section 3 to the account model in section 2: how an asset's properties become a haircut, why the route out of the asset decides more than the price chart does, where the asset sits in the liquidation order, and what happens to a levered account when a yield-bearing collateral asset is impaired.

## The two lines the whole page runs on

A margin account holds an owner, a set of positions, and a set of debts, and derives a single health factor across all of them.

```
collateralValue = Σ (quantity × price × (1 − haircut))
healthFactor    = collateralValue / totalDebt
```

Liquidation is triggered when `healthFactor < 1`.

Everything below is an answer to one of two questions those lines raise: what sets the haircut, and what order things get sold in when the health factor breaks.

## Haircuts

```
haircut = f(price volatility, enforcement certainty, unwind route)
```

Arch Prime sets the haircut on each asset. Three inputs, and they are genuinely independent — an asset can score well on one and badly on another.

**Price volatility.** How far the asset can move against you between the moment health breaks and the moment closeout completes. This is the input everyone models, and it is the one people mean when they say "risky asset."

**Enforcement certainty.** Whether the recovery process runs on time and without needing anyone's cooperation. On native Bitcoin collateral the answer is strong, and it is Arch Network's property rather than Arch Prime's: collateral remains an unspent transaction output on Bitcoin under predefined Taproot spending conditions, and seizure rights are capped, automatic, and dependent on nobody's cooperation. The scope boundary holds everywhere it is claimed: enforcement determines that closeout *begins*, not the price at which it *completes*.

**The unwind route.** How the asset is turned back into something that repays debt, how long that takes, and whether the path can be gated, suspended, or simply absent when it is needed.

**The unwind route is the input most often forgotten, and it has two forms that fail differently.** **Redemption is slow but contractual** — it runs on the issuer's stated terms, and those terms are where gates, notice periods, queues and suspension rights live. **Secondary depth is fast but conditional** — a sale clears immediately if there is a book, and the book is quoted by participants who withdraw it when volatility rises. A haircut is not a discount for volatility with a liquidity adjustment bolted on. If the redemption window is longer than the liquidation window and no depth is there, the volatility number stops being the binding term — the account is exposed for the whole duration of the wait, and the price at the end of the wait is not the price you saw when health broke.

This is where a yield-bearing asset diverges sharply from Bitcoin. Bitcoin's route out is a market question with a live answer at all hours. A strategy position has two possible routes and neither is guaranteed: a redemption process run by the asset's issuer, and a secondary market that may or may not have depth at the moment of stress. For Prime BTC and Prime Dollar the redemption process, the entity responsible for it, and any secondary depth are all unspecified.

## Liquidation rank, and the rank-1 requirement

Liquidation walks positions by liquidation rank ascending until health is restored. Arch Prime assigns the ranks: deployed strategy positions are rank 1, Bitcoin is rank 99, and Bitcoin is the last thing touched. When the walk does reach Bitcoin, moving it is Arch Network's step, not Arch Prime's — the spend requires a threshold signature the network produces.

That ordering is deliberate and it is the risk-migration statement made concrete. The capital efficiency in this account model comes almost entirely from counting the deployed position, not from netting — and the risk moves with it. Risk moves to the deployed leg, which is levered, which is why those positions are whitelisted, underwritten, and liquidated first. Reaching the client's Bitcoin is the last step, not the reflex.

Then the constraint that governs everything else on this page:

**A position cannot hold rank 1 unless it can be unwound inside the liquidation window, by either route** — sold on the secondary market, or redeemed with the strategy. The test is unwindability, not redeemability. A position with a deep secondary market can qualify despite long redemption terms; a position with neither a workable redemption window nor executable depth cannot.

This is not a preference. A rank-1 position is the one the engine reaches for while an account is underwater. If reaching for it returns "come back in 30 days" and there is no book to sell into, the rank is fictional and the account walks straight past it to the next rank — which, in an account whose only other asset is the client's Bitcoin, means Bitcoin gets sold to cover a shortfall the strategy leg created.

So the redemption SLA is not a service-level detail, and neither is the depth of the instrument's secondary market. Between them they determine whether a yield-bearing asset can carry a low rank at all, and therefore whether the ordering above protects the client's Bitcoin in practice or only on paper. An asset that qualifies on depth alone qualifies on the route most likely to be gone when it is needed.

## Concentration, and the V1 reality

V1 runs one strategy Arch Prime has whitelisted, which means 100% concentration by construction.

Stated plainly, because it should not be discovered later: an account deployed in V1 has all of its deployed exposure in a single strategy run by a single manager. There is no second strategy to fall back on and no netting across strategies, because V1 margin is deliberately additive and does no correlation modelling.

That is acceptable only because Arch Prime hand-underwrote the strategy, and it is not a precedent. Per-strategy and per-manager caps arrive in phase two.

Two consequences a reader should carry:

- **Holding both Prime BTC and Prime Dollar is not diversification.** Same manager, same strategy family. The spot legs differ; the thing that can impair them does not.
- **A single operational failure at the manager is an account-level event, not a position-level one.** With one strategy, strategy risk and portfolio risk are the same risk.

## What impairment does to a levered account

In a levered account the binding constraint is not Bitcoin's price. It is the deployed position's NAV.

For an account with equity `E` and deployed position `V`, the impairment that wipes equity is:

```
E / V
```

That is the whole result, and it is worth sitting with. Leverage raises `V` while leaving `E` roughly where it was. So the more levered the account, the smaller the strategy impairment that ends it. The impairment tolerance is not a property of the strategy. It is a property of how much leverage is stacked on top of it.

**Illustrative arithmetic — not a product parameter, and not a promise about any strategy:**

> 1 BTC at $100,000 posted as collateral. $500,000 deployed. $500,000 of debt.
>
> Equity is $100,000; the deployed position is $500,000; so `E / V` = 20%.
>
> A 20% impairment in the strategy wipes the client at full Bitcoin value — Bitcoin never moved.
>
> A 100% Bitcoin drawdown, by contrast, leaves the deployed position still covering the debt in full.

These are the same account. The asset everyone watches is the one that cannot end it, and the asset nobody watches is the one that can.

For orientation on where `V` comes from: a looped position's implied LTV is roughly 33% at 1.5×, 50% at 2.0×, roughly 67% at 3.0×, and 75% at 4.0×. Arch Prime sets max LTV in V1 at 80%, which puts a hard ceiling at 5.0×.

## Applying all of it to a yield-bearing collateral asset

What follows describes how an asset of this shape *would* be treated once Arch Prime makes it eligible as collateral. It does not assert that Prime BTC or Prime Dollar is eligible today.

> **[OPEN — whether Prime BTC or Prime Dollar is the V1 whitelisted strategy, and whether either is collateral-eligible in V1 or arrives with heterogeneous collateral in V2, is not settled · owner: Product / Risk]**

Put the four sections together for an asset of that shape held as collateral behind a levered position, and the picture is consistent:

1. **Its haircut would be undecided,** because the unwind route that drives the haircut is undecided in both of its forms.
2. **Its liquidation rank would be undecided,** because rank 1 requires unwindability inside the liquidation window by one route or the other, and neither the redemption window nor the executable depth is established.
3. **Its concentration is 100%** in V1, by construction.
4. **Its NAV is the term in `E / V` that ends accounts,** and a market-neutral strategy's yield can go to zero or negative without Bitcoin moving at all.

The conclusion this page exists to make concrete: **a yield-bearing collateral asset is a levered position's weakest point, not its strongest.** The yield is the reason to hold it and the NAV is the reason it can end the account, and those are the same leg. An asset that pays you is an asset with a profit and loss, and a profit and loss inside collateral is a second source of liquidation that the price chart of the underlying will not warn you about.

The reading that follows from that: treat the yield leg as risk you are adding, not as a cushion you are holding.

## What does not hold

- **Yield does not reduce collateral risk.** It adds a second impairment path. It is not a buffer against the first.
- **A NAV mark is not a sale price.** The account values a strategy position at NAV. If the unwind route taken is a sale, what the position realizes is what the book will pay, and the gap between the two is a loss the haircut was not sized for. See [4.3 Strategy and NAV risk](../04-risks/4.3-strategy-and-nav-risk).
- **Rank 1 is not protection.** It orders what gets unwound; it does not determine the price. Arch Prime's rules determine that closeout begins and in what order, and Arch Network's authorization determines that the resulting Bitcoin transaction settles. Neither determines the price at which closeout completes.
- **Bitcoin being rank 99 does not mean Bitcoin is untouched.** It means it is last. If the earlier ranks cannot be realised inside the window, the walk reaches it.
- **The `E / V` figure above is arithmetic, not a parameter.** It is not a risk limit, a target, or a statement about any strategy's actual loss distribution.
- **This page states no haircut, no liquidation rank, and no liquidation threshold.** Those are undecided or unreconciled and are flagged rather than estimated.
- **V1 does not net risk across positions.** It is levered exposure through isolated pools — a margin-account experience, with no correlation modelling and no netting. True portfolio margin arrives with V2.
