This page defines the terms used across the Arch Prime documentation in the sense this set
uses them, and says where that sense differs from the ecosystem's.

## Terms

The first two entries govern how every other entry is read. Nothing in this set uses bare
"Arch" to mean either of them.

**Arch Network** — The chain. It is the Bitcoin clearinghouse: its purpose is to control
the Bitcoin collateral and settle to the Bitcoin network, with no discretionary operator in
the path.
*Where it matters:* the actor is Arch Network wherever collateral is held, a Bitcoin
transaction is authorized, transactions are sequenced or prioritized, or settlement occurs.
The reference class is DTCC and comparable clearinghouses, used as an analogy for the
function only and never as a claim of equivalent legal status, regulation or guarantee.

**Arch Prime** — The prime brokerage. It is a business built on Arch Network, and the
network's anchor tenant. It is not the network, and the network is not it.
*Where it matters:* the actor is Arch Prime wherever credit is extended, a counterparty is
underwritten, an account or risk parameter is set, a strategy is listed, a spread is earned
or a fee is charged. Arch Network is open — nothing stops another prime brokerage, or any
other application, being built on it, and Arch Prime is the reference implementation rather
than the privileged one.

**Margin account** — The Arch Prime object created when a client posts native Bitcoin: it
holds an owner, a set of positions and a set of debts, and derives a single health factor
across all of them.
*Where it matters:* everything on Prime happens against the account rather than against an
individual position, so a change anywhere in it moves one number.

**Health factor** — `collateralValue / totalDebt`, where
`collateralValue = Σ (quantity × price × (1 − haircut))`; closeout is triggered when it falls
below 1.
*Where it matters:* it is the only number the system acts on. Everything else — haircuts,
prices, ranks — is an input to it.

**Haircut** — The discount Arch Prime applies to a position's value before it counts as
collateral, computed as
`f(price volatility, enforcement certainty, unwind route)`.
*Where it matters:* the unwind route is decisive and is the input most often forgotten. An
asset that is stable in price but has no working way out inside the liquidation window is not
lightly haircut.

**Liquidation rank** — The order, assigned by Arch Prime, in which positions are taken
during closeout: deployed strategy positions are rank 1, Bitcoin is rank 99, and positions
are walked ascending until health is restored.
*Where it matters:* Bitcoin is the last thing touched. A position cannot hold rank 1 unless
it can be unwound inside the liquidation window, by either route — the receipt sold, or the
position redeemed with the strategy. A position with a deep secondary market can hold rank 1
despite long redemption terms; a position with neither a workable redemption window nor
executable depth cannot hold it at all. The older redeemability-only test is superseded.

**Receipt** — The instrument representing a deployed strategy position, minted directly into
the margin account by the borrow-and-deploy bundle and registered there as a position.
*Where it matters:* the client never holds it in their own wallet, but it **is** a
transferable instrument — the enforcement property is possession, not transferability. Do
not describe it as non-transferable, and do not say "no transferable value" without the
possession qualifier: closeout by sale depends on the instrument being transferable. A
branded name for it is under consideration and not chosen; this set says *receipt*.

**Unwind route** — How a deployed position is turned back into something that retires debt.
There are two, and either can satisfy the rank-1 test: the receipt is **sold** — in many
cases closeout, deleveraging and client exit are a swap of the receipt on a DEX — or the
position is **redeemed** with the strategy and the proceeds retire the debt.
*Where it matters:* the two fail differently, which is why the haircut takes the route as an
input. Redemption is slow but contractual — the counterparty is the manager and the timing
is a term. Secondary depth is fast but conditional, and it withdraws under stress; a position
underwritten on depth alone is underwritten on the route most likely to be gone when it is
needed. A sale also realizes market price rather than NAV: the account is marked at NAV, and
the receipt fetches what the book will pay.

> **[OPEN — which unwind route is primary (sale of the receipt or redemption with the
> strategy), who selects it at closeout, and whether it varies by strategy, are not settled.
> Both routes are described here; neither is presented as the normal path · owner: Product /
> Risk]**

**Buying power** — The borrowing an account's posted collateral will support after haircuts,
which the client uses to deploy into listed assets and yield strategies.
*Where it matters:* it is bounded by the account's collateral and by the per-account borrow
cap, whichever binds sooner.

**Looping** — Building levered exposure by repeating borrow-and-deploy against the same
collateral base on a per-strategy basis, using multi-instruction execution.
*Where it matters:* in V1, this is where leverage comes from. It is not produced by a
portfolio margin engine netting your positions.

**Isolated pool** — A lending market whose collateral and debt are scoped to that market
rather than netted against the rest of the account.
*Where it matters:* V1 runs on isolated pools underneath while presenting one account and one
health factor on top. The presentation is unified; the risk underneath is not netted.

**Atomic bundle** — A single all-or-nothing transaction sequence: validate collateral and the
requested borrow, issue debt, deposit proceeds into the whitelisted strategy, mint the receipt
directly into the margin account, register it as a position, recompute health and assert it —
or revert everything.
*Where it matters:* the client never holds the borrowed funds. There is no step at which the
borrower has possession of the proceeds or of the receipt, which is enforcement by
transaction shape rather than by policy — the property is possession, not a restriction on
the instrument. The all-or-nothing property is Arch Network's — one execution environment,
one state transition; the steps inside the bundle are Arch Prime's.

**Pre-confirmation** — Agreement on Arch Network application state, reached before the
corresponding Bitcoin transaction is confirmed.
*Where it matters:* pre-confirmation is not Bitcoin finality. A transaction can leave the
mempool before confirmation, and a Bitcoin reorganization can alter recently confirmed state.
Agreement on Arch Network state does not by itself move BTC — authorization of the Bitcoin
transaction is a separate step, and conflating the two is the most common misreading of the
architecture.

**Threshold signing** — Arch Network's distributed signing authority, in which key shares are
held by validators in proportion to stake weight, using FROST threshold Schnorr signatures with
ROAST for robustness when signers are unavailable or adversarial.
*Where it matters:* no single operator — including Arch Network's own developers — can move
collateral unilaterally, and Arch Prime cannot move it either. FROST produces standard Schnorr
signatures, so settlement appears on Bitcoin as an ordinary Taproot-signed transaction with no
distinguishing marker. Both constructions are published, peer-reviewed work that Arch Network
did not invent; the network's contribution is operating them as the live authorization layer
over native Bitcoin collateral.

**Enforcement without possession** — The property **Arch Network** provides: the client keeps
custody of the Bitcoin, while the lender holds seizure rights that are capped, automatic, and
dependent on nobody's cooperation.
*Where it matters:* it is the network's property, not the brokerage's, and it is why **Arch
Prime** can extend credit against a self-custody account at all. Arch Prime consumes the
property; it cannot create it, and cannot extend it past the network's perimeter to assets
sitting at a third-party venue. Its boundary is that enforcement determines that closeout
begins and in what order, never the price at which it completes.

**Closeout** — The rule-based process that reduces debt when an account's health factor falls
below 1, walking positions by liquidation rank until health is restored.
*Where it matters:* the rule and the ordering are **Arch Prime's**; the authorization that
moves the Bitcoin when the walk reaches rank 99 is **Arch Network's**. Because the second half
runs on the network, closeout is not a negotiation and does not require the client's
cooperation or Arch Prime's continued existence. What it fixes is the trigger and the sequence,
not the route it takes at rank 1 and not the market it executes into.

**Redemption window** — The time a redemption with the strategy takes to settle in the asset
that repays debt.
*Where it matters:* it is one of the two unwind routes, not the test on its own. It feeds
both haircut and liquidation rank, and it is what a position falls back on when the receipt
has no market deep enough to clear inside the liquidation window. Long redemption terms are
not disqualifying by themselves.

**Liquidation window** — The time inside which a rank 1 position has to be unwound for the
closeout schedule to hold.
*Where it matters:* it is the bound the rank-1 test is measured against, on either route. Its
length is not specified anywhere in the corpus, so the test has no measurable value today.
See [Risk parameters](8.1-risk-parameters).

**Utilisation** — The share of committed lending depth that is currently lent out.
*Where it matters:* target utilisation is 85% and the rate curve inflects at 90%; the target
sits below the inflection to preserve withdrawal headroom for the liquidity providers funding
the book. It also sets what depth can serve: supportable user collateral is
`(committed depth × utilisation) ÷ (leverage − 1)`.

**Per-account cap** — The maximum an account may borrow, expressed as a share of available
depth rather than a dollar amount.
*Where it matters:* it is how capacity is rationed in the absence of a minimum account size.
A cap of 2% admits 50 accounts at full cap, so the cohort size is arithmetic
rather than discretionary.

**Market-neutral** — A strategy constructed to carry little directional exposure to the price
of the assets it trades.
*Where it matters:* it describes the construction, not the risk. See the note below.

**NAV (net asset value)** — The value of a deployed strategy position, which is what the
account holds after deployment.
*Where it matters:* in a levered account the binding constraint is not Bitcoin's price — it is
the deployed position's NAV. For an account with equity `E` and deployed position `V`, the
impairment that wipes equity is `E / V`.

## Where this set uses a term differently

**Cross-margin.** V1 is not a cross-margin account and must not be described as one. It gives
an experience that feels like a cross-margin account, but it runs on isolated pools underneath: the
client borrows against collateral, and leverage comes from multi-instruction looping on a
per-strategy basis. Margin is deliberately additive with no correlation modelling, so the
capital efficiency comes from counting the deployed position rather than from netting risk
across positions — which means risk moves to the deployed leg, which is levered, which is why
those positions are whitelisted, underwritten, and liquidated first. True cross-margining —
portfolio margin, heterogeneous collateral, netted risk — is V2.

**Market-neutral.** Elsewhere the term is often used to imply an absence of risk. It does not
mean that here. Neutrality is to direction, and it bounds nothing else: the strategy carries
execution, model, counterparty, venue and operational risk, and its yield can go to zero or
negative.

**Collateral.** Bitcoin posted to Prime is not wrapped, bridged or reissued on another chain.
It remains an unspent transaction output on Bitcoin, locked under predefined Taproot spending
conditions by Arch Network. Where other systems use "collateral" to mean a representation of
an asset held elsewhere, this set means the coin.

**Prime brokerage.** Used here for the function — financing, execution access, and credit
against a portfolio — rather than as a regulatory characterisation.

## What this glossary does not settle

- **Defined terms are not defined values.** "Liquidation threshold" has a meaning and no
  agreed number; the liquidation window has a role in the rank test and no stated length. See
  [Risk parameters](8.1-risk-parameters).
- **Naming both routes is not choosing between them.** "Unwind route" defines the two ways a
  position closes out. Which one runs, and who selects it, is flagged above and not settled.
- **Latency has no single number here.** Two distinct latencies exist — signing and
  coordination on Arch Network, and settlement on Bitcoin — and a figure for one is not a
  figure for the other.
- **Vocabulary is not scope.** A term appearing here does not mean the feature it names ships
  in V1.
