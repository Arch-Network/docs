---
title: The collateral that cannot be served
---


This page answers why Arch Prime exists: there is a large pool of Bitcoin collateral that
carries no leverage, and the reason it carries none is structural, not commercial.

## The gap

Bitcoin is a large pool of unlevered collateral, and it is largely unserved.

Not unserved because nobody wants the business. Institutions extending credit against
Bitcoin at scale have generally required the same thing before they will lend: the
collateral must leave the holder's own custody and sit with the lender, the lender's
affiliate, or a custodian the lender approves. That is the price of enforceability in a system where
enforcement means somebody has to be able to grab the asset.

The largest holders refuse to pay that price. For a long-horizon Bitcoin holder, moving
the coin is the risk. So the credit does not get extended, and the collateral sits idle —
not because the holder rejects leverage, but because the holder rejects the transfer that
every leverage provider demands.

## Off-exchange settlement moved the transfer, it did not remove it

The standard answer to this problem is off-exchange settlement: collateral is held away
from the trading venue, at a custodian, mirrored to the venue for margining purposes. It
is a real improvement on the alternative, and it solves a real problem — venue failure.

It does not solve this one. Off-exchange settlement relocated the transfer rather than
removing it. The coin still leaves the holder's own custody. It now sits with a third
party under a legal arrangement instead of with the exchange under a different legal
arrangement, and the holder is still relying on somebody else's solvency, controls and
cooperation to get it back. For the holder whose entire objection is *I do not hand over
my coin*, a better custodian is not an answer to the objection. It is a different party to
have the objection about.

## A lending protocol prices collateral. A prime broker prices a counterparty.

This is the structural distinction, not a quality claim.

An on-chain lending protocol looks at an asset, assigns it a haircut and a threshold, and
lends against it. It has no view on who you are, and it cannot have one — its
enforcement is a liquidation function and nothing else. That works, and it is why over-
collateralized lending markets exist. It also caps what can be built on top: every
position is a standalone loan against a standalone asset, and nothing in the design knows
that the positions belong to one client.

A prime broker does the opposite. It underwrites a counterparty — the account, its
positions, its debts, its behaviour under stress — and organizes credit, execution and
custody around that relationship. That is what makes a portfolio financeable rather than
a collection of loans.

Prime brokerage has never reached self-custody Bitcoin holders because underwriting a
counterparty has always implied controlling the counterparty's assets. Remove that
implication and the business becomes available to a client base it has never covered.

## What changes it

The property that removes the implication is enforcement without possession: the client
keeps custody, and the lender gets seizure rights that are capped, automatic, and
dependent on nobody's cooperation. That property comes from Arch Network, not from the
brokerage built on it. Collateral stays an unspent transaction output on Bitcoin under
predefined Taproot spending conditions, and the rights against it are exercised through
Arch Network's authorization layer, which no single operator controls.

That is enough for Arch Prime to underwrite against, which is why third-party lenders are
willing to fund a book of it. It is also why the marginal client costs nothing for Arch
Prime to onboard, and therefore why a small self-custody account can be served at all.

The boundary on the claim is fixed: enforcement determines that closeout begins, not the
price at which it completes.

## What this does not claim

- It does not claim the holder's risk is removed. It relocates from *will I get my coin
  back from a custodian* to *will Arch Network's enforcement behave as specified*. Those are
  different risks, not zero risk, and the second is covered in the risk section.
- It does not claim off-exchange settlement is defective. It is a good answer to venue
  failure and a poor answer to custody transfer.
- It does not claim lending protocols are inferior. They price a different thing.
- It carries no sizing. Any market-size or addressable-collateral figure you may have seen
  elsewhere in Arch Network or Arch Prime material is unsourced and is flagged above.
