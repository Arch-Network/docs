title: Atomic bundles

This page describes the transaction shape that makes Arch Prime work: one bundle that
issues credit, deploys it, and books the result, or does none of it.

Two entities are in that sentence and they do different halves of it. The atomicity is a
capability **Arch Network** supplies — one execution environment, one state transition, no
partially-applied state. What runs inside the bundle — the credit, the deployment, the
margin accounting — is **Arch Prime's**. This is the clearest concrete case of the general
point: the business is doing something here that it could not do for itself on a chain it
did not control.

## The bundle, step by step

Borrow-and-deploy happens as one atomic bundle. Six steps, one state change:

**1 · Validate.** The collateral in the account and the requested borrow are checked
against the registry parameters — haircut, borrow eligibility, account weight — before
anything is issued.

**2 · Issue debt.** The borrow is created against the account.

**3 · Deposit into the whitelisted strategy.** The proceeds go straight into the strategy
the borrow was requested against. They do not pass through a client-controlled address on
the way.

**4 · Mint the receipt directly into the margin account.** The claim on the deployed
capital is created inside the account, not delivered to the owner and then posted back. The
receipt is a transferable instrument; what the bundle guarantees is that the client never
has possession of it.

**5 · Register it as a position.** The receipt becomes a `Position` with an asset
identifier, a quantity and a liquidation rank, and from that moment it counts toward
collateral value.

**6 · Recompute health and assert it.** The health factor is recalculated across all
positions and debts and checked. If the assertion fails, the whole bundle reverts.

Nothing between steps 2 and 6 is observable as a completed intermediate state. Either the
account ends the bundle with a debt, a position and a health factor that passes, or the
account ends the bundle exactly as it started.

## The property this creates

The client never holds the borrowed funds. There is no step at which the borrower has
possession of the proceeds, or of the receipt that replaces them.

State it that way rather than as a claim about the instrument. The receipt is transferable —
closeout by sale depends on it being transferable — and the property the bundle creates is
possession, not transferability.

The rest is worth stating precisely, because it is the difference between this design and
the obvious alternative. A system could issue the loan, hand the proceeds to the borrower, and
require by rule that the borrower deposit them into an approved venue — then monitor,
penalise, and chase. That is a rule that has to be policed, and policing is where credit
systems fail.

Here the constraint is a state that never exists. There is no moment in the bundle at
which the proceeds are in the borrower's hands, so there is no moment at which they can go
somewhere else. This is enforcement by transaction shape, not by policy.

The scope boundary belongs in the same breath: enforcement of this kind determines that
the deployment happens as specified and that closeout begins when the rule says it does.
It does not determine the price at which any of it completes.

## Why it can be one bundle

Credit issuance, deployment into a strategy, and margin accounting are three different
programs' concerns. They sit in one bundle because Arch Network lets programs invoke one
another within the same execution environment — programs run in the ArchVM, an eBPF-based
environment written against in Rust, which indexes Bitcoin's chain and mempool so programs
can reference live Bitcoin state.

That is the network's capability, not the brokerage's. Arch Prime did not build the
property; it is a tenant of a chain that has it, and the shape of its product follows from
that. On infrastructure it did not control, the same three concerns would sit in separately
governed protocols with a coordination step between them, and the coordination step is
where the guarantee would be.

So the bundle spans credit, deployment and accounting without crossing a network boundary.
There is no bridge step, no message queue, no relayer, and no partially-applied state
sitting on one side of a hop waiting for the other side to acknowledge. Arch Network is a
Layer 1, an independent chain, and the whole sequence is one state transition on it.

For a builder this collapses an entire category of work. You are not writing compensating
transactions for the case where the deposit succeeds and the accounting does not, because
that case does not have a representation.

## Exit paths

There are three, and it is worth being exact about what they are, because a builder will be
asked to show them.

**Sell the deployed position to repay debt.** The receipt is swapped on a DEX and the
proceeds retire the borrow. What the account is left with is what the market paid, which is
not the same as the NAV the position was marked at.

**Redeem the deployed position to repay debt.** The position is redeemed with the strategy
and the proceeds retire the borrow. What the account is left with depends on what the
position was worth when it was redeemed.

**Repay from external funds and withdraw Bitcoin.** The borrow is retired with capital
brought in from outside, and the collateral is released.

There is no fourth path. In every one of the three the debt is retired before collateral is
released, and there is no route that extracts borrowed capital from the account. If your
product's flow diagram has a "withdraw borrowed funds" arrow, it is drawing a different
product.

Do not present either of the first two as the normal exit. Which one runs is undecided, and
timing on both is unsettled for V1:

## Two steps, not one, at the Bitcoin edge

A bundle that succeeds has produced agreement on Arch Network application state. That is
not the same as having moved BTC.

Agreement on Arch Network application state does not by itself move BTC. Authorization of the
Bitcoin transaction is a separate step, and conflating them is the most common misreading
of the architecture. When settlement does occur, it appears on Bitcoin as an ordinary
Taproot-signed transaction with no distinguishing marker, because the signing construction
produces standard Schnorr signatures.

Anything in your product that says "done" needs to know which of the two it is talking
about. See [Reading account state](7.4-reading-account-state).

## What you supply, what Arch Prime and Arch Network supply, what is not yet available

**You supply:** the user's intent and parameters, the presentation of the bundle's outcome
as a single result rather than a sequence, and the handling of a revert as a real outcome
rather than an error state to retry blindly.

**Arch Prime supplies:** the steps inside the bundle — validation against its registry
parameters, debt issuance, deployment into the strategy it has listed, receipt minting,
position registration, and the health recomputation and assertion.

**Arch Network supplies:** the execution environment that makes those steps one state
transition, the property that no intermediate state is reachable, and authorization and
settlement of the Bitcoin leg.

**Not yet available:** the callable construction of the bundle from an external
application, and the timing of either unwind route — the mechanics of the sale, and the
redemption terms — that would determine what a user can be shown about exiting.

> **[OPEN — the callable construction of the borrow-and-deploy bundle is not specified in
> this set: instruction names, ordering constraints, parameter shapes, and what a failed
> health assertion returns to the caller · owner: Protocol Engineering / Developer
> Relations]**

## What does not hold

- **Atomicity is not finality.** The bundle reverting or completing is a statement about
  Arch Network state. Pre-confirmation is not Bitcoin finality: a transaction can leave the
  mempool before confirmation, and a Bitcoin reorganization can alter recently confirmed
  state.
- **The bundle does not make the position good.** It fixes the accounting, not the
  strategy. Risk moves to the deployed leg, which is levered, which is why those positions
  are whitelisted, underwritten, and liquidated first.
- **The assertion is a check at one instant.** Passing at step 6 says the account was
  healthy at the valuations in force at that moment. The next valuation is a separate
  event.
- **This is not a general-purpose composition primitive documented here.** What a
  third-party program may compose with, and how, belongs to the developer book.
