title: Integrating with Arch Prime
---


This page tells an engineer at another protocol, wallet, exchange or application what
Arch Prime looks like from the outside, which three integration patterns exist, and which
of them you can build against in V1.

## Two different things you might be here to do

They are different relationships, with different counterparties, and this section covers
only one of them.

**Integrating with Arch Prime.** You surface a client's Prime account inside your own
product, route a user into originating or extending a position, or supply capital into the
closeout of a Prime position. Your counterparty is the business. Arch Prime owns the
account, the collateral registry and the risk parameters your integration reads and acts
on. **This is what section 7 documents**, and everything below is about it.

**Building your own application on Arch Network.** Arch Network is open. Nothing stops
another prime brokerage, or an application that is not a prime brokerage at all, being
built on it — Arch Prime is the anchor tenant and the reference implementation, not the
privileged one. Your counterparty there is the chain rather than the business, and none of
the Prime objects described below are the surface you would build against.

That second path is not documented in this set. It needs its own surface, and section 7 is
not it. Nothing here should be read as the terms on which the network is available.

> **[OPEN — there is no documentation surface for building an application on Arch Network
> rather than integrating with Arch Prime, and no governing document specifies what a third
> party must do: whether program deployment is permissioned, what the network charges for
> blockspace or settlement, and how an application other than Arch Prime obtains
> collateral-authorization rights · owner: Protocol Engineering + Product]**

## What you are integrating with

Arch Prime is the Bitcoin-native on-chain prime brokerage, and the infrastructure fact
that makes that sentence mean anything belongs to Arch Network, not to Arch Prime:
collateral remains an unspent transaction output on Bitcoin, locked under predefined
Taproot spending conditions, and is never reissued on another chain. Programs run in the
ArchVM, an eBPF-based environment written against in Rust, which indexes Bitcoin's chain
and mempool so programs can reference live Bitcoin state.

> **[OPEN — "prime brokerage" terminology pending counsel sign-off; working guidance is
> that the defensible phrasing may be "prime brokerage functions," and every page in this
> set using the term is reviewed as one set · owner: Legal / Counsel]**

From an integrator's point of view, Prime reduces to three things:

**1 · An account object, and it is Arch Prime's.** A margin account holds an owner, a set
of positions, and a set of debts, and derives a single health factor across all of them.
That derived number is what the system acts on, and it is the number your product will
most often want to show.

**2 · A transaction shape, supplied by Arch Network and used by Arch Prime.** Borrowing
and deploying happen as one atomic bundle that either completes in full or reverts. The
client never holds the borrowed funds. See [Atomic bundles](7.3-atomic-bundles).

**3 · An execution environment that is aware of Bitcoin, and it is Arch Network's.** Arch
Network is a Layer 1, an independent chain. Agreement on Arch Network application state
does not by itself move BTC; authorization of the Bitcoin transaction is a separate step.
Any surface you build has to represent that split honestly. See
[Reading account state](7.4-reading-account-state).

## The three integration patterns

### Read-only — surface a user's Prime account in your own product

You display a user's Prime account: positions, debts, the derived health factor, and how
close the account is to closeout. You write nothing.

This is the pattern with the least unspecified surface, because everything it needs is
already a V1 object. The account, its positions, its debts and the health factor exist in
V1 by construction, and the collateral registry parameters that feed the health
calculation exist alongside them.

Two things you must get right before you ship it. First, a health factor is only as good
as the price or NAV underneath it, and a stale input produces a number that is
arithmetically correct and materially wrong. Second, a position that is agreed in Arch
Network state and a position whose Bitcoin leg has settled are different states, and your
UI has to say which one it is showing. Both are covered in
[Reading account state](7.4-reading-account-state).

### Origination — route a user into opening or extending a position

Your product hands a user into Prime: post Bitcoin, open an account, borrow against it,
deploy into a strategy Arch Prime has listed, or add to an existing position.

The underlying mechanism exists in V1. Posting native Bitcoin generates a margin account,
and the borrow-and-deploy bundle is the V1 path for using it. What is not settled here is
the composition surface — whether and how a third-party application constructs, sponsors
or hands off that bundle from outside, and what the resulting attribution and economics
look like for you.

> **[OPEN — the origination surface for third-party applications is not specified: how an
> external product constructs or hands off a borrow-and-deploy bundle, and what
> attribution exists for referred flow · owner: Product / Protocol Engineering]**

> **[OPEN — take rate / financing spread does not exist anywhere in the corpus and is a
> named launch blocker; no figure, range or "approximately" may be quoted to an
> integrating partner · owner: Operating Frame owner / Finance]**

### Liquidation participation — supply capital into closeout

You run a liquidator: you watch for accounts whose health factor has fallen below 1, and
you supply the capital that repays debt in exchange for the liquidation buffer.

The trigger and the ordering are V1 mechanics — liquidation walks positions by liquidation
rank ascending until health is restored, deployed strategy positions are rank 1 and
Bitcoin is rank 99. What you cannot build against yet is the interface and the economics:
neither the keeper interface nor the size of the liquidation buffer is published. See
[Participating in liquidations](7.5-participating-in-liquidations).

> **[OPEN — external liquidator (keeper) interface not published: how a liquidator
> observes eligible accounts, submits, and is settled · owner: Protocol Engineering /
> Risk]**

> **[OPEN — liquidation buffer size not published, so liquidator economics cannot be
> modelled · owner: Risk]**

## Availability in V1

| Pattern | Underlying mechanism in V1 | What is missing before you can build |
|---|---|---|
| Read-only | Yes — account, positions, debts, health factor and registry parameters all exist | The callable read interface is documented in the developer book, not in this set |
| Origination | Yes — the borrow-and-deploy bundle is the V1 path | The third-party composition surface and the economics are unspecified |
| Liquidation participation | Yes — rule-based trigger and rank ordering exist | Keeper interface and buffer size are both unpublished |

Access is also staged. Committed lending dollars, not code, gate the launch: leverage
capacity is a direct function of stablecoin depth, so the launch is a capacity ladder, not
a date. Plan your integration timeline against a rung, not a release date. See
[Environments and where the reference lives](7.6-environments-and-where-the-reference-lives).

## What you supply, what Arch Prime and Arch Network supply, what is not yet available

**You supply:**

- Your own user identity, session and key handling. Prime accounts are owned by the
  client; your product does not take custody to integrate.
- Your own display of health, staleness and settlement state, with the two-step
  distinction between Arch Network state agreement and Bitcoin settlement made explicit.
- Your own risk copy. If your product shows a levered position, it has to tell the user
  what moves it, and in a levered account the thing that moves it is the deployed
  position's NAV.
- For liquidation participation: capital, execution, and the willingness to absorb
  execution risk.

**Arch Prime supplies:**

- The account model and the derived health factor.
- The collateral registry parameters — valuation source, haircut, max account weight,
  liquidation rank, borrow eligibility, redemption terms — that determine how a position
  counts.
- The rule-based liquidation trigger and its ordering.

**Arch Network supplies:**

- The execution environment, and with it the atomic bundle that makes borrow-and-deploy a
  single all-or-nothing state change.
- Authorization of any movement of the Bitcoin collateral, produced collectively rather
  than by any single operator.
- Settlement on Bitcoin through standard Schnorr signatures, which appear on Bitcoin as an
  ordinary Taproot-signed transaction with no distinguishing marker.

**Not yet available:**

- External venue connectivity. Out of scope for V1.
- Tokenized equities or securities-like collateral. Out of scope for V1.
- Correlation netting and cross-account netting. Out of scope for V1; true portfolio
  margin, heterogeneous collateral, netted risk and venue integrations arrive in V2.
- Rehypothecation of any kind. Out of scope for V1, and out of scope permanently unless
  explicitly reintroduced. If your integration assumes collateral can be re-lent, it does
  not fit this system.
- "Trade anywhere." It is not shipping. It is staged, and if you reference it in your own
  product surface you must present it as staged.

## What does not hold

- **This is not a trading integration.** Trading is not in scope for V1. What the user is
  trading is yield.
- **Do not model V1 as a cross-margin account.** It gives the user an experience that
  feels like one and is not one. Underneath it is levered exposure through isolated pools:
  users borrow against collateral, and leverage comes from multi-instruction looping of
  those assets on a per-strategy basis. If your product computes margin as if positions
  offset, it will be wrong. V1 margin is additive, with no correlation modelling.
- **V1 runs one whitelisted strategy**, which means 100% concentration by construction.
  That is acceptable only because the strategy is hand-underwritten, and it is not a
  precedent. Per-strategy and per-manager caps arrive in phase two. Any concentration view
  you build against V1 will need to change.
- **This page is not the callable reference.** Endpoint names, parameters, response
  shapes, error codes, SDK methods and CLI usage live in the Arch developer book. Nothing
  in section 7 should be treated as an interface contract.
