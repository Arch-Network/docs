title: Participating in liquidations

This page describes closeout from the outside: what triggers it, the order it walks, what
an external liquidator is being paid to do, and which parts of that are not yet published.

## The trigger is a rule, not a decision

Liquidation is triggered when `healthFactor < 1`. That is the whole condition. No desk
approves it, no committee reviews it, and no party has to cooperate for it to begin.
Enforcement is non-discretionary and survives Arch Prime's insolvency, because the leg
that matters does not run through Arch Prime: moving the collateral requires Arch
Network's authorization, not the business's cooperation.

The boundary on that claim is not a caveat, it is half the claim: enforcement determines
that closeout begins, not the price at which it completes. The rule fixes the trigger and
the sequence. The route taken and the depth available on the day determine what is
actually recovered.

For a liquidator that boundary is the business. You are not being paid to detect the
trigger — the trigger is arithmetic anyone can compute. You are being paid to supply
capital at the moment it is needed and to absorb the difference between the rule's timing
and the market's.

## The order it walks

Liquidation walks positions by liquidation rank ascending until health is restored.

- **Rank 1 — deployed strategy positions.** These are unwound first. This is deliberate,
  and it follows from where the risk sits: risk moves to the deployed leg, which is
  levered, which is why those positions are whitelisted, underwritten, and liquidated
  first.
- **Rank 99 — Bitcoin.** The client's posted collateral is the last thing touched.

Between the two, rank is assigned per registry entry, and one constraint governs it: a
position cannot hold rank 1 unless it can be **unwound** inside the liquidation window, by
either route. A position with a deep secondary market in its receipt can hold rank 1 despite
long redemption terms; a position with neither a workable redemption window nor executable
depth cannot hold it at all. Rank is therefore an unwindability property before it is a risk
preference, which is also why
`haircut = f(price volatility, enforcement certainty, unwind route)` treats the route as
decisive.

"Until health is restored" is the stopping condition. Closeout is sized to restore health,
not to close the account.

## Two routes out of a rank 1 position

This is the part that decides what you are actually doing when you liquidate.

**Sale.** The receipt for the deployed position is a transferable instrument — it is minted
into the margin account and the client never holds it, but the enforcement property there is
possession, not transferability. So closeout can be executed as a swap of the receipt on a
DEX: the instrument is sold, the proceeds retire the debt, and the strategy is never
contacted. This is fast, and it is conditional on a book being there.

**Redemption.** The position is redeemed with the strategy, the receipt is burned, and the
proceeds retire the debt. This is slow, and it is contractual — the timing is the manager's
redemption SLA rather than the market's depth.

Which of the two runs is not settled, and you should not build on the assumption that it is
one of them.

## What an external liquidator does

Closeout needs someone to stand on the other side of it. Arch Prime does not fund client
financing — third-party liquidity providers do — and the same logic runs through
liquidation: the capital that repays a failing account's debt comes from outside, and so
does the execution.

The external liquidator supplies two things:

**Capital, at the moment it is required.** The debt is retired now, not when the position
finishes unwinding.

**Absorption of execution risk.** The liquidator takes the position and takes what the
market gives them for it. If the route is worse than modelled, or the depth is not there,
that gap belongs to the liquidator, not to the lender and not to the protocol.

What that second job consists of depends on the route. On the sale route you are pricing an
instrument you will have to move in a book, and your exposure is the spread between the mark
and the fill, over minutes. On the redemption route you are pricing a claim that settles on
the manager's schedule, and your exposure is a carry — you hold a position you cannot exit
for the length of the SLA, in conditions that produced a liquidation. Those are different
trades with different capital requirements, and a liquidator built for one is not
automatically ready for the other.

What the liquidator receives in exchange is not specified. Whether a liquidation penalty is
charged to the account, and by what mechanism a liquidator is compensated for standing
ready, is not defined anywhere in the corpus, and it is that mechanism which determines
whether liquidators show up in the conditions where they are needed most.

Those two flags are the reason this page cannot yet be built against. The mechanism is
specified; the participation surface and its economics are not.

## What you are pricing: market price, not NAV

Where closeout runs as a sale, the position realizes a market price and not a NAV. This is
the economics of the trade, so state it in the sharpest form: the account is marked at NAV,
and the receipt fetches what the book will pay.

The gap between those two numbers is yours. It is not a case of the strategy being impaired —
the strategy can be exactly where its NAV says it is, and the sale can still print below the
mark — and it is not something the rule-based trigger bounds, because enforcement determines
that closeout begins, never the price at which it completes. It is a separate mechanism that
stacks on top of any impairment in the position itself, and a model that carries only one of
the two understates the loss.

Under a cascade it widens, and it widens for a structural reason rather than a sentimental
one: liquidations sell into the same book. The size arriving is correlated with the
conditions that thinned the book, so the depth you observed while modelling is not the depth
you will trade against.

The redemption route does not remove this; it relocates it. There you are not exposed to a
fill against a thin book, you are exposed to the NAV moving over the length of the SLA while
you cannot exit, and to the manager's own gating rights.

## What V1 concentration means for a liquidator

V1 runs one whitelisted strategy, which means 100% concentration by construction. That is
acceptable only because the strategy is hand-underwritten, and it is not a precedent —
per-strategy and per-manager caps arrive in phase two.

The consequence for anyone planning to liquidate: rank 1 across every account in V1 is the
same position. Accounts do not fail independently of each other, and the day you are most
likely to be called is the day every other account needs the same position unwound into
the same market. Model correlated arrival, not average arrival.

That compounds with the section above rather than sitting beside it. One position means one
book on the sale route and one manager on the redemption route, so both routes narrow at the
same time and for the same reason.

## What you supply, what Arch Prime and Arch Network supply, what is not yet available

**You supply:** capital, execution, venue relationships, your own valuation of the
position you are taking on — including your own view of what the receipt would sell for as
distinct from what it is marked at — a model that works on both routes rather than one, and
your own tolerance for the gap between the rule's timing and the market's depth.

**Arch Prime supplies:** the rule-based trigger, the rank ordering that determines what is
unwound and in what sequence, and the registry parameters that set rank and haircut.

**Arch Network supplies:** the authorization layer in which key shares are distributed in
proportion to stake weight, so no single operator — including the network's own
developers — can move collateral unilaterally, and the settlement of the Bitcoin leg when
the walk reaches rank 99.

**Not yet available:** the keeper interface, how a liquidator is compensated, the
liquidation window duration, the liquidation threshold, the mechanics of the sale route, and
which route is primary or who selects it. All of them are flagged above.

## What does not hold

- **Rule-based closeout is not a recovery promise.** It is a claim about the certainty and
  timing of the recovery process, never about the price at which recovery occurs. A
  liquidator who reads it as a floor has misread it.
- **Rank 99 does not mean Bitcoin is untouchable.** It means it is last. In a large enough
  impairment the walk reaches it.
- **NAV is not a price.** The mark on the account is what the position is valued at, not
  what it will sell for. Do not use the account's collateral value as a bid.
- **Depth is not a parameter.** Where the sale route depends on a market for the receipt,
  that market is a condition, not a term anyone owes. It can be present in every observation
  and absent on the day you are called.
- **Restoring health is not closing the account.** The account continues, with less
  position and less debt.
- **Nothing here is a keeper contract.** No interface, submission format, timing window or
  settlement path is specified in this set, and none should be inferred from the mechanism
  described above.
