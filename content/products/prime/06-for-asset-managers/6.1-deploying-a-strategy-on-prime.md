This page answers what changes about your strategy when Arch Prime clients can deploy
into it with borrowed money.

Start with the consequence, because it determines whether the rest of this section is
relevant to you: **a position cannot hold liquidation rank 1 unless it can be unwound
inside the liquidation window — by either route.** There are two. The receipt for the
position can be sold, or the position can be redeemed with you and the proceeds applied to
the debt. A strategy with long redemption terms can still qualify if the receipt has a deep
enough secondary market to clear inside the window. A strategy with neither a workable
redemption window nor executable depth cannot qualify at all.

Rank 1 is the rank a deployed strategy position holds in a Prime margin account. So your
redemption terms are not a commercial detail that gets negotiated late, and neither is the
market for your receipt. Between them they decide whether your strategy can be used with
leverage at all.

Everything below follows from that.

## What a Prime client is doing when they deploy into you

A client posts native Bitcoin as collateral, which generates a margin account. The
account creates buying power. The client borrows against that buying power and deploys
the proceeds into a whitelisted strategy.

Borrow-and-deploy happens as one atomic bundle. In a single transaction the system:

1. validates the collateral and the requested borrow,
2. issues the debt,
3. deposits the proceeds into the whitelisted strategy,
4. mints the receipt directly into the margin account,
5. registers the receipt as a position,
6. recomputes the account's health factor and asserts it.

If any step fails, the whole bundle reverts. There is no partial state in which the debt
exists and the position does not.

Two things follow that are unusual if you are used to taking subscriptions from
discretionary money.

**The client never holds the borrowed funds.** There is no step at which the borrower has
possession of the proceeds. That is enforcement by transaction shape, not by policy — the
funds move from the credit facility into your strategy without passing through anything
the client controls.

**The receipt is never in the client's wallet.** It is minted into the margin account and
registered there as a position. The client is the owner of that account, but the position
is inside the collateral perimeter from the moment it exists.

Be precise about what that does and does not say. The receipt is a transferable instrument.
The enforcement property is possession, not transferability — the client simply never has
custody of it. That distinction is load-bearing rather than pedantic, because closeout by
sale depends on the instrument being transferable.

There are three ways out, and the debt is retired before collateral is released in every
one of them: the position is sold — the receipt swapped on a DEX; the position is redeemed
with you and the proceeds retire the debt; or the client repays from external funds and
withdraws their Bitcoin. There is no path that extracts borrowed capital from the account.

So your subscriber is not a fund investor who happens to have a loan elsewhere. Your
receipt is a line in someone's margin account, and its value is an input to a health
factor that is recomputed continuously.

## Your position is liquidated first

A margin account holds an owner, a set of positions, and a set of debts, and derives a
single health factor across all of them:

```
collateralValue = Σ (quantity × price × (1 − haircut))
healthFactor    = collateralValue / totalDebt
```

Liquidation is triggered when `healthFactor < 1`. When it is, the engine walks positions
by liquidation rank ascending until health is restored. Deployed strategy positions are
rank 1. Bitcoin is rank 99. Bitcoin is the last thing touched.

That ordering is deliberate and it is the reason this section exists. The capital
efficiency gain in a Prime account comes almost entirely from counting the deployed
position, not from netting risk across positions — V1 margin is additive and there is no
correlation modelling. Risk moves to the deployed leg, which is levered, which is why
those positions are whitelisted, underwritten, and liquidated first.

Read that as an operational fact about your book, and note that it lands on you differently
depending on which route the unwind takes. If closeout runs by redemption, Prime accounts
present redemptions to you at exactly the moment your strategy is least likely to want
them. If it runs by sale, no request reaches you at all — your receipt changes hands in the
market, and what you see is your register moving and a price printing against your NAV.
Either way it happens before anyone touches the Bitcoin.

## Why the unwind route is the binding term

Enforcement on Arch Network is non-discretionary: collateral remains an unspent
transaction output on Bitcoin under predefined Taproot spending conditions, and seizure
rights are capped, automatic, and dependent on nobody's cooperation. The client retains
custody; enforcement does not require possession.

The boundary on that property matters to you. Enforcement determines that closeout
begins, not the price at which it completes. The engine can begin unwinding a rank 1
position on schedule; whether the proceeds arrive depends on you.

A haircut is a function of three inputs:

```
haircut = f(price volatility, enforcement certainty, unwind route)
```

The unwind route is decisive, and it is the input most often forgotten. It has two forms
and they fail differently. Redemption is slow but contractual: the timing is what you
committed to, and the obligation is yours. Secondary depth is fast but conditional, and it
withdraws under stress — the book that would have taken your receipt on a quiet day is
thinner on the day it is needed. A strategy underwritten on depth alone is underwritten on
the route most likely to be gone when it is needed.

So a strategy whose economics are excellent, whose redemption is slow, and whose receipt
has no market is not a strategy with a slightly worse haircut. It is a strategy that cannot
sit at rank 1, which means it cannot be the thing a levered account is holding, which means
it cannot be deployed into with borrowed money at all.

## What V1 actually is, so you can calibrate

V1 runs one whitelisted strategy. That means 100% concentration by construction: every
levered client account holds the same deployed position.

That is acceptable only because the strategy is hand-underwritten, and it is not a
precedent. Per-strategy and per-manager caps arrive in phase two. If you are reading this
as a template for how strategies get added, it is not one — the underwriting was bespoke,
and the concentration it permits is a property of there being exactly one of them.

> **[OPEN — per-strategy and per-manager concentration caps for phase two are not sized; there is no published maximum account weight a manager can plan against · owner: Risk]**

Two other V1 boundaries worth stating plainly:

- V1 gives a margin-account experience but uses isolated pools underneath. Clients borrow
  against collateral, and leverage comes from multi-instruction looping of those assets on
  a per-strategy basis. It is levered exposure through isolated pools, not portfolio
  margin. True portfolio margin, heterogeneous collateral and netted risk are V2.
- Trading is not in scope for V1. The client is trading yield. Your strategy is the
  product they are expressing a view on.

## What you supply, what Arch Prime supplies, what does not exist yet

**You supply:** the strategy itself; a redemption SLA you can meet under stress; what you
know about the secondary market for the receipt, including how it has behaved when the
market was not calm; a valuation Arch Prime can verify independently; the disclosures in
[6.5](./6.5-reporting-and-disclosure-obligations); and the operational capacity to
handle forced redemptions arriving in a cluster rather than a queue.

**Arch Prime supplies:** the margin account and its health arithmetic; the registry entry
and its risk parameters; the liquidation engine that will call you first; and the credit
facility, which is funded by third-party liquidity providers rather than by Arch Prime's
own balance sheet.

**Arch Network supplies:** the execution environment in which borrow-and-deploy runs as a
single atomic bundle, the authorization of any movement of the Bitcoin collateral, and
settlement of that leg to the Bitcoin network.

**Not yet available:** the route by which a strategy becomes listed. There is no
published listing programme, no submission interface, and no specification you can build
against today.

> **[OPEN — the process by which a strategy becomes available on Prime is not a ratified, published programme; there is no submission path, interface specification or listing timeline a manager can be pointed at · owner: Product]**

The term "prime brokerage" is used in this section as shorthand for the functions
described — a margin account, financing against posted collateral, and rule-based
closeout — running on Arch Network, an independent Layer 1 that indexes Bitcoin's chain
and settles to it.

> **[OPEN — use of the term "prime brokerage" is pending counsel sign-off; working guidance is that the defensible phrasing may be "prime brokerage functions" · owner: Counsel]**

## What does not hold

- Nothing here is an offer to list a strategy, and nothing here describes an approval you
  can rely on. The one whitelisted V1 strategy was underwritten by hand.
- Rank 1 is not a status you can request. It is a consequence of the position being
  unwindable inside the liquidation window by one route or the other.
- Depth in the receipt's market is not a term you can commit to. It is a condition that
  holds until it does not, and it is not equivalent to a redemption obligation you owe.
- Arch Prime does not underwrite your strategy's returns to clients, and does not stand
  behind your NAV. If your strategy impairs, the client's equity absorbs it before
  anything else does.
- Enforcement is a property of the collateral leg, not of your leg. It determines the
  timing and certainty of the recovery process, never the price at which recovery occurs.
