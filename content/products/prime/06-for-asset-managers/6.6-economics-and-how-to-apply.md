This page answers who earns what between you, Arch Prime and the client, and what to do if
you want a conversation about deploying a strategy on Prime.

Two of the three commercial terms on this page have not been decided. They are flagged
rather than estimated, because a manager cannot model a business against a number that was
invented to fill a gap.

## The three revenue lines

There are three parties and three distinct claims on the same flow.

**You charge the client.** Management fee and performance fee, on your own schedule,
disclosed in full before deposit ([6.5](./6.5-reporting-and-disclosure-obligations)).
Arch Prime does not set your fees.

**The lender earns interest.** Arch Prime does not fund client financing. Third-party liquidity
providers do. The interest the client pays on borrowed dollars is the cost of that
capital, and it is the client's cost, not yours — but it is subtracted from the same gross
return your strategy produces, which is what makes the client's hurdle higher than an
unlevered investor's.

**Arch Prime earns a spread.** Arch Prime earns a spread on flow it does not finance.
Because Arch Prime is not underwriting each client off its own balance sheet, the marginal
client costs nothing to onboard — which is why Prime can serve a small self-custody account
when a balance-sheet prime cannot.

Those three stack on one gross return. The client's net is what remains after your fees,
the financing cost, and Arch Prime's spread — amplified by leverage in both directions. A
strategy whose gross return is thin does not become viable at 4× leverage; it becomes a
strategy where the financing cost consumes the spread and the client discovers it in a
drawdown.

## What has not been decided

**The take rate does not exist.** The financing spread — what Arch Prime charges over the
cost of lender capital — is not defined anywhere in the corpus. It is a named launch blocker
and a diligence blocker. There is no figure, no range, and no approximation to offer.

> **[OPEN — the take rate / financing spread does not exist anywhere in the corpus; it is a named launch blocker and a diligence blocker, and no figure, range or approximation may be stated · owner: Finance + Operating Frame owner]**

**The fee split has not been decided.** How manager fees, financing cost and Arch Prime's
spread divide — including whether Arch Prime takes a share of manager fees at all, and how
a performance fee interacts with a levered position that can be force-unwound
mid-period — is undecided.

> **[OPEN — the fee split between manager, lender and Arch Prime has not been decided, including whether Arch Prime takes any share of manager fees and how performance fees are treated on a position closed by liquidation · owner: Product + Finance]**

Note what the second one implies operationally: a forced unwind closes a position at a
moment neither you nor the client chose, and it may do so by a sale of the receipt rather
than a redemption with you — in which case the position leaves the account without a
subscription event ever reaching your desk. Whether a performance fee accrues, reverses or
is waived in either case is not settled, and you should not assume the answer your own
documents would give.

## What determines how much flow reaches you

Not marketing. Depth.

Committed lending dollars, not code, gate the launch. Leverage capacity is a direct
function of stablecoin depth, so the launch is a capacity ladder rather than a date:
R0 closed beta at up to $10M of depth, R1 first cohort at $25M, R2 scale-up at $50M,
R3 open with caps at $100M, R4 uncapped at $250M and above. The served-collateral and
account-count figures modelled against that ladder are models, not forecasts, and are not
published here.

Two formulas govern what that depth supports:

```
supportable user collateral = (committed depth × utilisation) ÷ (leverage − 1)
```

A client seeking leverage λ consumes (λ − 1) dollars of borrow per dollar of collateral.
Target utilisation is 85%; the rate curve inflects at 90%, and 85% preserves withdrawal
headroom.

Rationing is by per-account borrow cap expressed as a share of available depth, starting at
a recommended 2% and stepping down as depth grows. A 2% cap admits 50 accounts at full
cap. There is no minimum account size and no minimum ticket — a minimum would contradict
the reason Prime exists.

The practical reading for a manager: subscription sizes at the early rungs are bounded by
committed depth and per-account caps, not by your capacity. Size your expectations against
the rung, not against the addressable market.

Four gates open a rung, and one of them is directly about this section: committed and
papered depth, where indications do not count; security, meaning a second audit, formal
verification of the liquidation path and a funded bug bounty; verification, meaning
readiness evidence with a named verifier; and disclosure, which requires the take rate
defined, the strategy product reconciled, and risk parameters correct in every public
surface. Strategy listing is gated on that last one, and the take rate flag above is part
of it.

## How to apply

Honestly: there is not yet a programme to apply to. There is no submission portal, no
published criteria, no committed review timeline, and no stated decision authority. The
V1 strategy was whitelisted through a bespoke, hand-run underwriting, and no repeatable
path has been ratified behind it.

> **[OPEN — the application path for asset managers is not a ratified programme: no submission channel, published criteria, review timeline, decision authority or notification of outcome exists, and there is no named point of contact to publish · owner: Product]**

What exists today is a conversation. It is with Arch Prime's risk function on eligibility
and parameters, and with its product function on listing and disclosure — the same two
functions that own the flags throughout this section. Until the path above is ratified,
the useful thing a manager can do is arrive with the material assembled, because the
material is the same whatever process it eventually enters.

## Preparation checklist

Everything here is something you can produce today, and everything here will be asked for.

**The strategy**

- [ ] Yield source described and mapped to cash flows originating outside Arch Prime
- [ ] Instruments, markets and direction of exposure
- [ ] Capacity, how it is measured, and behaviour at the ceiling
- [ ] Historical drawdown: depth, duration, cause
- [ ] What the strategy is hedged against, and what it is not

**Redemption**

- [ ] Redemption window you can meet in the bad state, stated in wall-clock terms
- [ ] Every gate, suspension, notice extension and in-kind right in your governing
      documents, including ones never used
- [ ] Settlement asset and the operational path by which cash reaches an account
- [ ] What happens when many redemptions arrive at once, described concretely

**Valuation**

- [ ] Publication cadence, including weekends, holidays and venue outages
- [ ] Method: what is marked to market, what to model, sources, sign-off
- [ ] The independent verification a third party could reproduce
- [ ] What is published when the method cannot be run

**Counterparties and custody**

- [ ] Who holds assets at each step, under what legal arrangement
- [ ] Every venue touched, and which sit outside Arch Network's enforcement perimeter
- [ ] What happens to client property if any of those parties fails

**Commercial and legal**

- [ ] Full fee schedule, including any charge that applies on a forced redemption
- [ ] Governing documents, jurisdiction, regulator, auditor
- [ ] The transfer and redemption mechanics of the position asset an account will hold

**Arithmetic**

- [ ] `E / V` at each leverage level a client might use, so you know the drawdown that
      wipes them — the arithmetic is on
      [6.3](./6.3-underwriting-and-risk-parameters)

## What you supply, what Arch Prime supplies, what does not exist yet

**You supply:** the checklist above, and your own fee schedule.

**Arch Prime supplies:** the client relationship, the margin account, the credit facility
funded by third-party liquidity providers, the registry entry and its parameters, and the
liquidation engine.

**Arch Network supplies:** the clearing layer underneath all of it — control of the
Bitcoin collateral, and settlement to the Bitcoin network.

**Not yet available:** the take rate, the fee split, and the application programme. All
three are flagged above, and none should be assumed.

## What does not hold

- No commercial term on this page is committed. Two are undecided and flagged; the third
  is your own.
- The capacity ladder describes gating logic and committed depth, not a forecast of flow.
  Modelled account counts are not published and should not be inferred.
- Being prepared is not being accepted. There is no process today that converts a complete
  submission into a listing.
- Arch Prime's spread is earned on financing flow it does not fund. It is not a fee for
  distributing your strategy, and it should not be modelled as one.
