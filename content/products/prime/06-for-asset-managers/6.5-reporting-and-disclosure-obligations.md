This page answers what you have to publish before a client can deploy into your strategy,
and what you have to keep publishing afterwards.

The obligations below are not a compliance exercise bolted onto the product. Your NAV is
an input to a health factor, your unwind terms are an input to a liquidation schedule, and
your disclosures are what a client reads while deciding how much leverage to take against
them. Reporting here is part of the machinery.

## NAV

**A stated cadence.** How often the valuation is published, in wall-clock terms, including
what happens at weekends, holidays and venue outages. The engine recomputes health
continuously; a NAV published slowly means the account is running on a stale number
between publications, and the interval is priced into your haircut.

**A stated method.** How the value is computed: what is marked to market, what is marked
to model, which sources are used, how illiquid or unpriced holdings are treated, and who
signs off. The method is the disclosure. A number without a method cannot be verified, and
a valuation the risk engine cannot verify independently is a valuation it cannot use.

**A stated failure mode.** What is published when the method cannot be run — a stale mark,
a suspension, an estimate — and how the difference is signalled.

## Strategy brief

A plain description of what the strategy does and how it makes money, written for someone
deciding whether to borrow against it.

- The yield source, mapped to cash flows that originate outside Arch Prime.
- The instruments and markets used, and the direction of exposure.
- What the strategy is hedged against, and what it is not. If you describe the strategy as
  market-neutral, the disclosure must say what that covers: a hedge against directional
  price movement and nothing else. Execution, model, counterparty, venue and operational
  risk remain, and the yield can go to zero or negative.
- Capacity, and what happens at the ceiling.
- Historical drawdown — depth, duration and cause.

Performance figures are attributed to your own books and presented as yours. Arch Prime
does not adopt a manager's track record and does not present one as its own.

## Risk disclosures the client sees before depositing

A client deploying into your strategy in a Prime account is doing it with borrowed money,
so the disclosures have to be legible in that context, not in the context of an
unencumbered allocation. At minimum the client must see, before depositing:

- the redemption terms, including every gate, suspension, notice extension and in-kind
  settlement right in your governing documents, whether or not you have used them;
- the transfer mechanics of the receipt, and any restriction that would prevent it being
  sold, since a sale of the receipt is one of the two ways the position can be closed out;
- the drawdown that would impair the position, stated as a percentage, so it can be read
  against their own leverage;
- the valuation cadence and method, so they know how stale the number in their account can
  be;
- the custody and counterparty model, below;
- the fee schedule, below.

## The secondary market for the receipt — an obligation that has not been written

If closeout can run as a sale of the receipt rather than a redemption with you, then the
market for that receipt is load-bearing in the same way your redemption SLA is. It is
reasonable to expect a manager's obligations to extend to it: what is known about depth, how
it is observed, and notice when it deteriorates.

That expectation has not been turned into a requirement, and this page will not invent one.
Nothing in the corpus says whether a manager must report on the receipt's market, what would
be reported, or on what cadence — and unlike a redemption term, it is a market condition
rather than something a manager controls, which is part of why the obligation is hard to
write.

> **[OPEN — whether a manager has any reporting obligation in respect of the receipt's
> secondary market — what must be disclosed about depth, who observes it, on what cadence,
> and what must be notified when it deteriorates — is unspecified; no such obligation exists
> today · owner: Product / Risk]**

What is already required is narrower and does exist: disclose the transfer mechanics of the
receipt and anything that would restrict its sale, and notify promptly when those change.

## Custody and counterparty model

Who holds the assets at each step, under what legal arrangement, and what happens to
client property if any of those parties fails. Name the categories of counterparty, the
jurisdictions, and the venues.

Then state the boundary, in the client's language, without softening it:

**Where a strategy routes assets to a venue outside Arch Network's enforcement perimeter,
the network's enforcement property does not extend there. Saying so is a disclosure Arch
Prime requires of you.**

Those are two separate things and it is worth keeping them separate. The perimeter is the
network's: Arch Network's property on the collateral leg is enforcement without
possession — the client keeps custody of their Bitcoin, which remains an unspent
transaction output under predefined Taproot spending conditions, and the seizure right is
capped, automatic and dependent on nobody's cooperation. That property is a consequence of
where the asset sits and how it is locked. It does not travel with the money. Assets moved
to a third-party venue — an exchange, a broker, a custodian, a counterparty balance
sheet — are held under that venue's terms and are exposed to that venue's failure, and no
part of Arch Network's architecture changes that.

The obligation is Arch Prime's. It cannot move the network's perimeter and does not try
to; what it requires instead is that the boundary is stated where a client will read it.
So if your strategy does this, the disclosure has to say so directly: which venue, what
share of assets, under what arrangement, and what a client's claim looks like if the venue
fails. A client should not have to infer it from a diagram.

## Fee schedule

The full schedule, in one place: management fee and how it accrues, performance fee with
its basis, hurdle, high-water mark and crystallization frequency, and every other charge —
subscription, redemption, transaction, administration — including any charge that applies
on a forced redemption. A redemption fee that only bites during a liquidation is a fee the
client pays at the worst moment, and it has to be visible before they deposit, not
discovered in the unwind.

## Notification obligations

These are continuing, and they are the ones most likely to be missed.

**Strategy deviation.** Any material change to what the strategy does, the instruments it
uses, the venues it touches, or its risk limits — before it takes effect where possible,
promptly where not.

**Capacity changes.** Reaching, approaching or revising capacity. Prime flow arrives
levered, so capacity is consumed faster than the equity behind it suggests.

**Anything that impairs a route out.** Any gate, suspension, extension, in-kind settlement
or expected delay on the redemption route — immediately, and before it is exercised where
the mechanism allows notice. The same obligation applies to a change in the receipt's
transfer mechanics that would restrict or block a sale. This is the highest-priority
notification in this section, because between them the two routes are what determines
whether client Bitcoin gets sold. See
[6.4](./6.4-redemption-terms-and-liquidation-rank).

**Anything that changes an underwriting answer.** Custody arrangements, counterparties,
auditors, key personnel, valuation method, or governing documents.

## What you supply, what Arch Prime supplies, what does not exist yet

**You supply:** every item above, in a form a third party can reproduce, and on a schedule
you keep.

**Arch Prime supplies:** the surfaces where a client encounters your strategy in the
product, the registry parameters derived from your disclosures, and the client-side risk
pages that describe strategy and NAV risk generically.

**Not yet available:** the specification for all of it — the format, the minimum cadence,
the schema, the delivery channel, and the notification deadlines.

> **[OPEN — there is no published reporting specification: no disclosure template, no NAV publication schema or delivery channel, and no stated minimum valuation cadence · owner: Product]**

> **[OPEN — notification deadlines are undefined: how quickly a manager must report a strategy deviation, a capacity change, a redemption impairment or a restriction on transferring the receipt, and to whom, is not specified · owner: Risk]**

> **[OPEN — responsibility for surfacing manager disclosures to clients before deposit — what the product displays, what the manager publishes, and who reviews accuracy — has not been assigned · owner: Product + Counsel]**

## What does not hold

- Publishing a disclosure does not transfer the risk it describes to the client. It makes
  the risk visible; it does not make it acceptable.
- Arch Prime's review of your disclosures is not diligence performed on a client's behalf,
  and Arch Prime does not verify your NAV on an ongoing basis beyond what the registry
  entry requires.
- Arch Network's enforcement does not extend to venues outside its perimeter, and no
  disclosure changes that. Disclosure states the boundary; it does not move it.
- Nothing here substitutes for the obligations you owe your own investors under your own
  regulator and governing documents.
