title: Launch phases
---


This page explains how Arch Prime opens to users: what defines each phase, what has to be
true before the next one starts, and why none of it is expressed as a date.

## The launch is a capacity ladder, not a date

The ladder is Arch Prime's. It governs how much credit the business can extend and how
widely it opens accounts, not what Arch Network does — the network's programs run whether or
not a rung is open.

Committed lending dollars, not code, gate the launch. Leverage capacity is a direct function
of stablecoin depth, and a dollar of depth serves less collateral the more leverage the
cohort takes, so how many accounts Prime can carry is set by how much credit has been
committed to the book — not by when the software is finished.

That is why the launch is described as a ladder with five rungs rather than a sequence of
release dates. A rung opens when the conditions for it are met. If depth arrives sooner, the
rung opens sooner. If it does not, the rung does not open, and no amount of engineering
progress substitutes.

## The rungs

Each rung is defined by the committed depth behind it. What changes as you climb is how much
committed depth stands behind the book, and how widely access opens.

| Rung | Committed depth | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| **R0 · closed beta** | ≤$10M | Access by invitation only. |
| **R1 · first cohort** | $25M | Access widens beyond the beta to a defined first cohort. |
| **R2 · scale-up** | $50M | More depth behind the book, and access widens further. |
| **R3 · open with caps** | $100M | Access is open, against the published per-account cap. |
| **R4 · uncapped** | $250M+ | The widest access on the ladder. |

The column says only what the gameplan supports: the depth threshold that gates the rung and
the direction access moves. How the per-account cap behaves at each rung is not set.

> **[OPEN — the per-account borrow cap of 2% of available depth is a recommended starting
> value, not a ratified parameter, and the step-down schedule as depth grows is not set, so
> no rung on this ladder can state what the cap is at that rung · owner: Risk]**

Two things about that table.

The depth figures are the gating thresholds that define each rung. They are not a fundraising
schedule and they are not a forecast of when depth arrives.

The gameplan also carries modelled served-collateral and account-count figures for each rung.
Those are model output, not commitments, and they are deliberately not reproduced here.
Publishing a modelled account count as a forecast would turn an internal sizing exercise into
a promise about how many clients get served, which is exactly the kind of number that gets
quoted back later.

## What does not change as you climb

The ladder adds capacity. It does not add product scope.

- V1 stays V1 at every rung. External venue connectivity, tokenized equities or
  securities-like collateral, correlation netting, cross-account netting, and rehypothecation
  of any kind remain out of scope. Rehypothecation is out of scope permanently unless
  explicitly reintroduced.
- The account remains levered exposure through isolated pools. It is not a cross-margin
  account at any rung; true portfolio margining is V2.
- V1 runs one whitelisted strategy, which means 100% concentration by construction.
  Per-strategy and per-manager caps arrive in phase two, not at a higher rung of this ladder.
- "Trade anywhere" is not shipping at any rung on this ladder. It is staged.

## The four gates

A rung opens when four gates are satisfied. All four, not a majority.

They do not all belong to the same party. Depth and disclosure are Arch Prime's to close.
Security is a gate on Arch Network's implementation. That matters when reading a status
report: progress on one is not progress on the other, and neither party can close the
other's gate.

**1 · Depth.** Committed and papered depth at the threshold for the rung. This is the gate
that is most often reported as met when it is not. An indication of interest is not
committed depth. A term sheet under negotiation is not papered depth. A relationship with a
large balance sheet is not depth at all. The gate is money that is committed and documented,
and nothing softer counts toward it.

**2 · Security.** A second audit, formal verification of the liquidation path, and a funded
bug bounty. This gate is on Arch Network's implementation rather than on Arch Prime's
commercial readiness: what is audited and verified is the network's programs and the
liquidation path they run. The liquidation path is singled out because it is the mechanism
the entire lender-side proposition rests on.

**3 · Verification.** Readiness evidence, with a named verifier attached to it. Evidence
that nobody is named against is a self-assessment.

> **[OPEN — the verifier who signs the readiness evidence for each rung is not named in the
> corpus; the verification gate cannot be evidenced until they are · owner: Operations]**

**4 · Disclosure.** Take rate defined, strategy product reconciled, and risk parameters
correct on every public surface.

## Where the disclosure gate stands

Stated plainly, because a reader can check it: the disclosure gate is not currently
satisfied, and each of its three parts is open.

> **[OPEN — the take rate / financing spread is undefined across the entire corpus and is a
> named launch and diligence blocker; the disclosure gate cannot be closed until it is set ·
> owner: CEO — Operating Frame §5]**

> **[OPEN — the strategy product is not reconciled: the vault under construction is a
> trusted-manager vault with materially different custody and counterparty properties to the
> vault model described in quarantined source files, and the public description cannot be
> finalised until product settles which one ships · owner: Product]**

> **[OPEN — risk parameters are not correct on every public surface: max LTV is contested
> across the corpus and the liquidation threshold has no agreed value, so the derived bands
> on existing surfaces inherit the error · owner: Risk]**

## What does not hold

- **No rung carries a date.** Nothing on this page states or implies when a rung opens.
- **The rungs are not entitlements.** Reaching a depth threshold satisfies one gate of four.
- **Climbing the ladder is not a change to the network.** A rung opening says Arch Prime has
  capacity to extend more credit. It says nothing about Arch Network's validator set,
  signing threshold or upgrade authority, none of which the ladder addresses.
- **Depth can go down.** The ladder is described upward because that is the plan, not because
  movement is one-way. Capacity follows committed depth in both directions.
- **Access at a rung is still rationed.** Open access at R3 means open applications against a
  published per-account cap, not uncapped borrowing.
