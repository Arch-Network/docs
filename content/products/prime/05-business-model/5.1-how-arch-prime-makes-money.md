title: How Arch Prime makes money

This page answers where Arch Prime's revenue comes from, what Arch Prime has to put at risk
to earn it, and which part of the answer is not settled yet.

## The mechanism

Arch Prime does not fund client financing. Third-party liquidity providers do. Arch Prime
earns a spread on flow it does not finance.

That is the whole revenue model in one sentence, and the order of the words matters. The
dollars a client borrows against Bitcoin come from lenders who supply that credit. Arch
Prime operates the account, sets the risk parameters, and lists the strategy that the
borrowed dollars are deployed into; the closeout it relies on runs as programs on Arch
Network. It takes a share of the financing rate for doing so. It does not take the credit
exposure that sits underneath.

> or collateral authorization, and how that relates to what Arch Prime charges a client —

## Why third-party capital funds the book

Stated as one thing rather than three: rule-based enforcement is why third-party capital is
willing to fund the book, third-party funding is why there is no balance-sheet constraint,
and the absence of that constraint is what makes the economics work.

Lenders underwrite the enforcement, not the asset. They are not forming a view on Bitcoin's
price and they are not extending credit to a name they have diligenced. They are
underwriting whether their claim executes when the account breaches — on time, in a known
order, without needing Arch Prime or the client to cooperate.

The property that supports that is enforcement without possession. The client keeps
custody. The collateral stays an unspent transaction output on Bitcoin, locked under
predefined Taproot spending conditions and never reissued on another chain. The lender's
seizure rights are capped, automatic, and dependent on nobody's cooperation, because key
shares over Arch Network's authorization layer are distributed in proportion to stake weight
and no single operator — including Arch Network's own developers — can move collateral
unilaterally.

One boundary travels with that claim every time it is made: enforcement determines that
closeout begins and in what order, not the price at which it completes.

## What this changes about the business Prime is

Two consequences follow, and they are the reason the economics differ from a
balance-sheet prime broker's.

**Growth is not rationed by Arch Prime's capital.** Because Arch Prime is not underwriting
each client off its own balance sheet, the marginal client costs nothing to onboard. That is
why Prime can serve a small self-custody account when a prime broker funding the book from
its own balance sheet cannot. It is also why there is no minimum account size — see
[Capacity, caps and why there is no minimum](5.3-capacity-caps-and-no-minimum).

**There is no concentrated counterparty book to lose.** A balance-sheet prime broker's
revenue is compensation for warehousing counterparty exposure. When a large client fails,
the loss lands on the broker. Arch Prime's revenue is compensation for operating the account
against the enforcement path Arch Network runs, and the credit exposure sits with the
liquidity providers who chose to take it, sized by rules published in advance.

Neither consequence is a claim that risk has gone away. It has moved, and
[Who funds the book](5.2-who-funds-the-book) names where it went.

## Market context

Global prime brokerage equity-finance revenue is a large annual pool set against a much
larger stock of hedge-fund gross borrowing, and it is overwhelmingly net interest margin —
which is why the business concentrates in a handful of banks and why it periodically
detonates. The figures behind that shape are industry aggregates. They are not Arch Prime's
revenue, Arch Prime's book, or a projection of either, and they stay in the flag below until
a source is attached.

> **[OPEN — the industry aggregates behind this paragraph — roughly $37B a year of global
> prime brokerage equity-finance revenue against $6.2T of hedge-fund gross borrowing — have
> no named published source in the corpus and may not appear on a public page until
> Research attaches one · owner: Research]**

The structural point in those numbers is that the incumbent model earns net interest margin
by funding clients off its own balance sheet, which is exactly the exposure that
concentrates the industry and periodically destroys participants in it. Arch Prime earns a
spread on flow it does not finance. That is a different exposure, not a smaller version of
the same one.

## The take rate does not exist yet

The central number on this page is missing, and it is missing on purpose rather than by
omission.

The take rate — the share of the financing spread Arch Prime retains — is not defined
anywhere in the governing documents. There is no figure, no range, and no approximation to
give. It is a named launch blocker and a diligence blocker in the Operating Frame, and "take rate
defined" is one of the four gates that has to be satisfied before a rung of the capacity
ladder opens. See [Launch phases](5.4-launch-phases).

Fee terms are not final. No rate quoted to any client, in any conversation, is a
commitment until this decision is made and published.

> **[OPEN — the take rate / financing spread Arch Prime retains is undefined across the entire
> corpus; no figure, range or approximation may be published or quoted until it is set ·
> owner: CEO — named launch and diligence blocker, Operating Frame §5]**

## What does not hold

- **This is not a claim that Arch Prime bears no risk.** Arch Prime does not carry the client
  credit exposure. It does carry the operational risk of running a business on top of Arch
  Network's enforcement path, the reputational consequence of a failure in it, and the
  business risk that lenders stop supplying depth.
- **No balance-sheet constraint is not unlimited capacity.** Growth is gated by committed
  lending depth, which is a real and currently binding limit.
- **A spread on flow requires flow.** Revenue is a function of borrow volume and
  utilisation, neither of which exists yet at scale.
- **The model is unproven until the take rate is set.** Until then this page describes a
  structure, not a rate card.
