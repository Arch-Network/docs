title: Strategy and NAV risk
---


This page explains why the deployed strategy — not Bitcoin — is the position most likely
to take a levered client's equity, and what a client is exposed to inside it. There are two
loss channels here, not one: the position can be impaired, and the position can be sold for
less than the account marked it at. They stack.

## Risk migrates to the deployed leg

When you borrow against your Bitcoin and deploy, the account does not end up holding two
comparable exposures. It ends up holding a small equity position in Bitcoin and a large
position in whatever the borrowed dollars were deployed into.

The mechanism makes this unavoidable. The borrowed funds never reach you: the bundle
issues the debt, deposits the proceeds into the whitelisted strategy, mints the receipt
directly into the margin account, and registers it as a position. The receipt is a
transferable instrument, but you never have possession of it — the enforcement property is
possession, not transferability — and no path extracts borrowed capital from the account.
Exit is by selling the deployed position, by redeeming it with the strategy, or by repaying
from external funds and withdrawing your Bitcoin. In all three the debt is retired before
collateral is released.

And the capital efficiency of the account comes almost entirely from counting that
deployed position, not from netting — V1 margin is deliberately additive, with no
correlation modelling. So: risk moves to the deployed leg, which is levered, which is why
those positions are whitelisted, underwritten, and liquidated first.

Liquidation walks positions by rank ascending until health is restored. Deployed strategy
positions are rank 1. Bitcoin is rank 99. Bitcoin is the last thing touched — because it
is the thing the client came to keep, and because it is the leg that was never the
problem.

## The arithmetic

For an account with equity `E` and a deployed position of value `V`, the impairment in
that position that wipes the equity is:

```
E / V
```

`V / E` is the leverage. So the wipeout impairment is the reciprocal of leverage, and it
falls as fast as leverage rises:

| Leverage | Deployed position vs equity | Impairment that wipes equity |
|---|---|---|
| 2.0× | 2× | 50% |
| 3.0× | 3× | ≈ 33% |
| 4.0× | 4× | 25% |
| 5.0× | 5× | 20% |

At the 5.0× ceiling implied by an 80% max LTV, a one-fifth impairment in the deployed
strategy is a total loss of client equity.

## Worked illustration

*Illustrative arithmetic only. These are not product parameters and no figure here is a
statement of what any account will be permitted to do.*

An account posts 1 BTC as collateral, with Bitcoin at $100,000. It borrows and deploys
$500,000, carrying $500,000 of debt.

- **A 20% impairment in the deployed strategy** removes $100,000 of value. The client's
  equity is gone, with Bitcoin still trading at its full $100,000.
- **A 100% Bitcoin drawdown** removes $100,000 of value. The deployed leg alone still
  covers the debt in full.

The two events produce the same dollar loss. One of them is Bitcoin going to zero. The
other is a strategy having a bad quarter.

That is the asymmetry, stated as plainly as it can be: this account is five times more
sensitive to the deployed leg's NAV than to Bitcoin's price, because the deployed leg is
five times the size of the equity. The client came in worried about the wrong number.

## Why a 20%-scale impairment is a bigger threat than a 100% Bitcoin drawdown

The dollar losses in the illustration are equal. The probabilities are not, and they run
the opposite way to intuition.

- **Frequency.** A 20% NAV impairment is a normal event class for an actively managed
  strategy — a model regime break, a venue failure, a funding-rate reversal, a
  concentrated position that gapped, an operational error, a fraud. Strategies produce
  losses of that scale regularly. Bitcoin going to zero is a different order of claim.
- **Observability.** Bitcoin's price is continuously quoted across many independent
  venues. Strategy NAV is computed periodically, often by or on behalf of the party whose
  performance it measures. You will see the Bitcoin move as it happens. You may see the
  NAV move considerably later. See [4.6](./4.6-price-and-oracle-risk).
- **Reachability.** Bitcoin can be sold into a market that exists at all hours. The
  deployed leg has to be *unwound* inside the liquidation window, by one of two routes,
  and each has a condition attached: redemption runs on the strategy's terms, and a sale
  needs a book to sell into. A position that can be unwound by neither route inside that
  window cannot hold rank 1 at all. See
  [4.4](./4.4-liquidity-and-redemption-risk).
- **Correlation of the failure modes.** The impairment, the exit pressure on both routes,
  the widening of whatever the strategy trades, and the queue of other clients trying to
  get out are one event, not four.

## What NAV risk is, specifically

NAV enters the account as the `price` term in the collateral computation. Everything
downstream — collateral value, health factor, whether liquidation triggers — is computed
from it.

NAV can be wrong in ways a traded price cannot:

- **Mark-to-model legs.** Positions with no observable market are valued by a method, and
  the method is chosen by the manager.
- **Accrual timing.** Unrealized gains recognised early and unrealized losses recognised
  late produce a NAV that is smooth and wrong in a specific direction.
- **Fee and financing accruals.** Costs that have been incurred but not booked overstate
  the position.
- **Staleness.** A NAV struck on a cycle is a statement about a past moment carried
  forward into the present.

The direction of the error is what matters. An understated NAV liquidates you early and
costs you. An overstated NAV does something worse: it keeps the health factor high while
the position is already impaired, so nothing triggers, the leverage silently exceeds the
parameters that were underwritten, and by the time the number moves the `E / V` arithmetic
has already run. The failure mode is not a NAV that jumps around. A jumpy NAV gets a large
haircut. The dangerous one is a NAV that is smooth, plausible, and flattering.

The haircut is set as a function of price volatility, enforcement certainty, and the unwind
route. It is not set as a function of reporting integrity.

## Closeout by sale realizes market price, not NAV

This is a second loss channel, and it is not the one above. Everything to this point
describes the position being *worth less*. This describes the position being *sold for
less than it is worth on the books*, with the strategy performing exactly as advertised.

The account is marked at NAV. `collateralValue` is computed from NAV, the health factor is
computed from `collateralValue`, and every parameter in the account is expressed against
that mark. But a receipt sold into the market fetches what the book will pay. Those are two
different numbers produced by two different processes: one is struck by whoever computes
the NAV, the other is set by whoever is bidding at the moment the position has to go.

The gap between them is not a valuation error. It is the price of immediacy, and it is
paid by the account, in full, at closeout.

Three things make it worse rather than better in the state where it is realized:

- **It widens under stress.** The bid for an instrument thins as volatility rises, while
  the NAV mark — struck on a cycle, by a process, from positions — does not move at the
  same speed or in the same direction. The mark stays orderly while the book empties.
- **Liquidations sell into the same book.** Under a cascade the account is not the only
  seller of that instrument. Other accounts breaching on the same move are unwinding the
  same position into the same depth, and each sale moves the price the next one gets. The
  discount to NAV is widest exactly when the largest number of positions have to realize
  it.
- **Nothing in the account bounds it.** The haircut discounts the mark; it does not bound
  the price the mark realizes. There is no parameter in this system that bounds realized
  price, and no enforcement property that creates a buyer.

It stacks on top of impairment rather than substituting for it. A strategy can be impaired
*and* its receipt sold at a discount to the impaired NAV, and the account bears both. The
`E / V` arithmetic above measures the first. It does not measure the second, and the two
are not alternatives.

This channel exists only on the sale route. Redemption realizes the strategy's own terms
rather than the market's — which is the argument for it, and the reason its own failure
mode is the slow, contractual one described in
[4.4](./4.4-liquidity-and-redemption-risk). Which route a closeout takes is not settled.

## Reflexive yield is disqualifying under leverage

A yield source is reflexive when the return depends on the system's own activity — token
emissions, incentive programs, fees generated by the very leverage the yield attracts, or
a return paid out of new inflows.

Reflexive yield is not merely lower quality than it appears. Under leverage it is
structurally unusable, for a reason that has nothing to do with the honesty of the people
running it:

1. NAV falls, for any reason.
2. Health falls, and liquidation begins.
3. Liquidation unwinds the position.
4. The unwind removes the conditions the yield was priced on. If the route is redemption,
   capital leaves the strategy and the activity the return depended on shrinks with it. If
   the route is a sale, the buyer is pricing the same shrinking return, so the bid falls
   and the receipt trades at a widening discount to the mark.
5. NAV falls further, or the executable price does, or both. Return to step 2.

The yield disappears precisely when the position must be exited, and the exit itself
removes the conditions that produced the yield. Neither route escapes it — they fail in
different currencies, one in redemption proceeds and one in price. The unwind is
self-reinforcing, and leverage sets how fast it runs. A strategy whose return is a function of its own assets
under management cannot be levered against on Arch Prime, and no haircut fixes it — a
haircut prices a level, and this is a feedback loop.

## Market-neutral does not mean risk-free

Where a listed strategy is described as market-neutral, that describes its exposure to
direction. It does not describe its exposure to loss.

Market-neutral strategies carry execution risk, model risk, counterparty risk, venue risk
and operational risk. The yield can go to zero. The yield can go negative. Directional
neutrality is achieved by holding offsetting positions, which means two sets of
counterparties, two sets of venues, and a basis that can move against the position while
neither leg does anything wrong.

## What a strategy must satisfy to be listed

Described generically, because the specifics of the strategy product are unresolved:

- It must be unwindable inside the liquidation window — sold or redeemed — to hold rank 1.
  The test is unwindability, not redeemability: a position with a deep secondary market can
  qualify despite long redemption terms, and a position with neither a workable redemption
  window nor executable depth cannot. If neither route works, it cannot be the position the
  system reaches for, and the account's structure changes.
- It carries a haircut set from price volatility, enforcement certainty, and the unwind
  route.
- It is whitelisted, which is a decision Arch Prime makes, and hand-underwritten, which is a
  process rather than a rule.

## Who bears it

The client bears NAV impairment down to zero equity, and bears the gap between NAV and
realized price on top of it if the position is closed out by sale. Below zero equity, the
shortfall is a credit loss to the third-party liquidity providers who financed the
position; Arch Prime does not fund client financing.

In V1 the client also bears 100% concentration in a single strategy, by construction. See
[4.5](./4.5-counterparty-risk).

## What reduces it

- Whitelisting and hand underwriting, which are a filter applied before listing rather
  than a control applied during a loss.
- The haircut, which values the position below its NAV.
- Rank 1 ordering, which reaches the levered leg before the Bitcoin.
- The atomic bundle, which prevents the borrowed funds from reaching anywhere other than
  the whitelisted strategy, and which asserts health before the position exists.

## What does not reduce it

- **Self-custody of the Bitcoin.** The Bitcoin is not what is at risk in this failure
  mode. Retaining custody of the collateral is irrelevant to what happens inside the
  strategy.
- **Enforcement certainty.** Rule-based enforcement determines that closeout begins, not
  what the position is worth when it completes, and not what a buyer will pay for it. It
  does not create a bid and it does not open a gated redemption window.
- **Diversification.** V1 margin is additive with no correlation modelling, so the engine
  gives no diversification credit — and with one whitelisted strategy there is no
  diversification to give credit for.
- **The liquidation rank.** Reaching the deployed leg first is only useful if the deployed
  leg still has value. Rank determines order, not recovery.
- **A haircut.** It absorbs a bounded valuation error. It does not detect a wrong NAV and
  it does not survive an impairment larger than itself.
- **Marking the account at NAV.** The mark is what the account computes health from. It is
  not what the position realizes on the way out, and the difference is borne by the client
  rather than by the parameter.

## The residual

A client at the leverage ceiling loses their entire posted Bitcoin on a one-fifth
impairment in a strategy whose NAV they cannot independently verify, on a reporting cycle
that has not been specified, in a product whose custody and counterparty model is under
reconciliation.

If the closeout runs by sale, the loss is larger than that arithmetic, by the distance
between the NAV the account was marked at and the price the receipt fetched — a distance
nothing in the system bounds, measured at the moment other liquidations are selling the
same instrument into the same book.

That is the position as it stands today, stated without adjustment.
