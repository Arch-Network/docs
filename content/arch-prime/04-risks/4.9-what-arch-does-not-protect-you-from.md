title: What Arch Prime does not protect you from
---


This page lists what a Prime account leaves you exposed to. It contains no mitigations, and
it is written to be read on its own.

## Two perimeters, and they are not the same one

Arch Network's enforcement perimeter and Arch Prime's control are different boundaries, and
an item can sit outside one, the other, or both.

**Arch Network's enforcement perimeter** covers a narrow and specific set of things. Your
collateral remains an unspent transaction output on Bitcoin, locked under predefined Taproot
spending conditions, and is never reissued on another chain. The lender's seizure rights over
it are capped, automatic, and dependent on nobody's cooperation. The borrowed funds never
reach you, and there is no path that extracts borrowed capital from the account. The health
computation and the liquidation walk run as programs rather than as decisions, so a closeout
begins when the rule says it begins.

That is the perimeter, in full. It is a mechanism for making a recovery process certain and
time-bounded, and it is a set of statements about *possession and process*.

**Arch Prime's control** is narrower still, and different in kind. It underwrites the
counterparty, whitelists the strategy, sets the haircut, the cap and the rank, and operates
the account. Those are judgements made in advance, not controls that operate during a loss.
Arch Prime does not hold your collateral, does not authorize a Bitcoin transaction, and
cannot make a validator set behave.

Everything on the list below is a statement about *value*, and none of it is addressed by
either paragraph above.

## The list

The items divide, and the division tells you who, if anyone, could ever address one. Items 1
to 6, 10 and 11 sit outside Arch Network's enforcement perimeter and outside Arch Prime's
control alike. Items 7 to 9 sit *inside* Arch Network — they are ways the network itself
fails, not things it declines to reach. Items 12 and 15 are Arch Prime's decisions and Arch
Prime's unclosed gaps. Items 13 and 14 are yours: one is a choice you make, the other is what
a levered account is.

**1. Price.** Enforcement determines that closeout begins, not the price at which it
completes. Bitcoin can fall far enough and fast enough to take the whole of your posted
collateral, and a liquidation that executes exactly on schedule can execute far below the
level at which it triggered.

**2. Strategy performance.** The deployed strategy can lose money. Where it is described as
market-neutral, that describes direction, not loss: execution, model, counterparty, venue
and operational risk all remain, and the yield can go to zero or negative. In a levered
account, an impairment of `E / V` — equity over deployed value — takes all of your equity.
At the 5.0× ceiling implied by an 80% max LTV, that is a 20% impairment.

> **[OPEN — max LTV of 80% is contested across the corpus and the 5.0× ceiling derived from it inherits that contest; not publishable as a parameter until reconciled · owner: Risk]**

**3. Execution quality.** Slippage, depth consumed, the timing of a forced exit, and the fee
market at the moment of settlement. Your position's quoted price is not its executable
value, and in a closeout you are a price-taker by design.

**4. Third-party venues.** For any strategy that routes assets to a venue outside Arch
Network's enforcement perimeter, the enforcement property does not extend there. What you
hold at that point is a claim, and its value depends on that venue's solvency, its
controls, its treatment of client assets, and its willingness to honour a redemption during
stress.

**5. Stablecoin issuers.** archUSD wraps dollars that already arrived. Its value depends on
the issuers of the stablecoins held in reserve — their solvency, their reserve composition,
their willingness to redeem, and their ability to freeze addresses. A break at a reserve
issuer passes straight through.

**6. Custody of the archUSD reserves.** Those reserves are custodied at BitGo. Custodian
insolvency, operational failure, key compromise or legal process against assets held is not
something Arch Network's programs reach, and not something Arch Prime's underwriting of the
strategy bears on.

**7. The validator set above threshold.** If participants controlling more than the signing
threshold collude, they can authorize transactions the rules do not permit. The resulting
spend appears on Bitcoin as an ordinary Taproot-signed transaction with no distinguishing
marker, and Bitcoin will accept it.

**8. Liveness.** Below the participation threshold, authorization halts. That stops
unauthorized movement and it also stops authorized movement — your withdrawal, your
repayment, and the liquidation that was supposed to protect the account while the market
kept moving.

**9. Upgrade authority.** Upgrade authority over Arch Network's programs is a real central
point of failure. Whoever holds it can change the collateral rules, the health computation,
the haircuts, the liquidation ranks and the conditions under which collateral moves. This is
a power over the rules, and it is not the same thing as Arch Prime setting a parameter
inside them. Every enforcement statement in this documentation holds given the programs as
currently deployed and not otherwise.

**10. Oracle and NAV inputs.** A stale or manipulated input produces a correctly executed
transaction against an incorrect assessment. Enforcement fidelity does not extend to input
fidelity. A NAV struck by the manager whose performance it measures reaches your health
factor the same way a market price does.

**11. Bitcoin itself.** Your collateral is an output on Bitcoin, so anything that affects
Bitcoin affects your collateral directly. Pre-confirmation is not finality. A transaction can
leave the mempool before confirmation. A reorganization can alter recently confirmed state,
including state a liquidation or a position opening was computed against.

**12. Concentration.** V1 runs one whitelisted strategy, which is 100% concentration by
construction. That is Arch Prime's listing decision, not a property of the network. There is
no per-strategy cap and no per-manager cap in V1; those arrive in phase two. Every failure
mode of that one manager applies to every deployed dollar in the system at the same time.

**13. Your own choice of leverage.** There is no minimum account size and no minimum ticket,
and there is nothing that stops you selecting a leverage level whose wipeout impairment is
smaller than the ordinary variation of the strategy you are deploying into.

**14. Loss of the account.** Bitcoin is liquidation rank 99 and is the last thing touched.
That determines the order in which positions are closed. It does not determine whether the
account can be closed at a total loss of your equity, and it does not create a floor.

**15. What is not yet decided.** The custody and counterparty model of the V1 strategy Arch
Prime has listed is under reconciliation, and until it is resolved you cannot determine from
this documentation where deployed assets sit or who holds them.

## How to use this page

If any single item on the list above is one you cannot absorb, the position to hold is
Bitcoin, unlevered.

None of these items is scheduled to be removed from this page. Items 1 through 14 are
structural — they follow from what a levered position is and from what Arch Network can and
cannot enforce. Closing the open flags in this section will make the risks better specified. It
will not make them smaller.

## The residual

Every item above is residual by construction. This page lists what is left after each
control described elsewhere in this section has worked exactly as designed, which is why it
names no mitigations: there are none to name against these. Stated once, plainly: a client
can lose the entire Bitcoin position they posted, at a price no parameter in this
documentation described, and nothing here bounds that loss below the size of the account.
