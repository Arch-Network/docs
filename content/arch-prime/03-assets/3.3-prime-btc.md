This page explains what Prime BTC is, where its yield comes from, what "market-neutral" does and does not mean, and which of its mechanics have not yet been decided.

## What it is

Prime BTC is a derivative asset that gives spot exposure to Bitcoin and also carries yield.

Two things are happening in one instrument, and it is worth separating them before going further:

**The spot leg.** Prime BTC tracks Bitcoin. If Bitcoin falls forty percent, that shows up.

**The yield leg.** Prime BTC also accrues a return from a trading strategy. That strategy has its own profit and loss, independent of Bitcoin's direction.

A holder is therefore carrying two exposures simultaneously. They are not correlated in any modelled way, they can move against you at the same time, and V1 margin does no correlation modelling — it is deliberately additive. Nothing in the account nets one leg against the other.

Which entity issues Prime BTC, and which stands behind the strategy book, is not stated anywhere. Arch Network holds Bitcoin collateral and settles to Bitcoin; Arch Prime extends credit and lists strategies. Issuing this instrument is neither function, and this page names no responsible party for it.

## Where the yield comes from

The yield on Prime BTC comes from market-neutral, HFT quant-driven strategies managed by Velox Trading.

> Arch has signed an LOI to acquire Velox, a seven-person quantitative trading firm, with close expected in [Q]. Velox's execution and risk capability is described throughout on the basis of its own historical books.

> **[OPEN — expected close quarter for the Velox transaction not stated · owner: Corp Dev]**

Velox brings the two prime brokerage functions that cannot be written in code: execution and a risk desk, with connectivity across roughly 60 venues. Arch Network, the infrastructure underneath the account, is separate and unaffected by that: collateral remains an unspent transaction output on Bitcoin, locked under predefined Taproot spending conditions, and is never reissued on another chain. The strategy is where the return comes from; Arch Network is where enforcement comes from. They are different systems and they fail for different reasons.

No performance figure for Velox appears on this page or anywhere in this set. That includes execution-quality claims.

## What "market-neutral" means, and what it does not

This term does more work in marketing than it can bear, so here is the precise reading.

**What it means.** The strategy is not taking directional exposure. Its return is not intended to come from Bitcoin going up, or from any asset going up. That is a statement about the *source* of the return.

**What it does not mean.** It is not a statement about the *size* of the loss distribution. Market-neutral does not mean risk-free. A strategy with no directional view can still lose money, and the ways it does are well understood:

- **Execution risk.** A high-frequency strategy's return lives inside the spread it captures. Slippage, latency, a venue outage mid-position, or a fill that does not arrive turns a modelled edge into a realised loss.
- **Model risk.** The relationship the strategy is trading can stop holding. Neutrality is a property of a model's assumptions, not a property of the world; when the assumptions break, the position is directional and nobody chose it.
- **Counterparty risk.** Positions and balances sit with venues, brokers and custodians. A counterparty that fails, freezes withdrawals, or claws back a fill imposes a loss the strategy did not trade into.
- **Venue risk.** Connectivity across roughly 60 venues is a source of opportunity and a source of surface area. Outages, halted markets, changed rules, delistings and settlement failures are venue events, not market events, and neutrality does not address them.
- **Operational risk.** A seven-person firm is a small number of people, systems and keys. Errors, key-person departure, and control failures are live risks and are not diversified away by the strategy being neutral.

**The bound, stated plainly.** The yield on Prime BTC can go to zero. It can go negative. A negative yield leg reduces the value of the instrument, and it does so whatever Bitcoin's price is doing. There is no floor under the yield leg described anywhere in this set, and this page will not imply one.

## What has not been decided

Each of the following is a separate undecided item with a separate consequence. None of them is a drafting gap.

**Accrual method.** Whether yield accrues by a rising exchange rate against Bitcoin or by rebasing the holder's unit balance is not decided. This changes what your balance looks like, how gains are measured, and how integrating systems account for the position.

> **[OPEN — accrual method for Prime BTC and Prime Dollar not decided: exchange-rate versus rebasing · owner: Product]**

**Mint and redemption process.** How Prime BTC is created and how it is returned is not decided — who can do it, against what, through what interface.

> **[OPEN — mint and redemption process for Prime BTC and Prime Dollar not decided · owner: Product]**

**Redemption SLA.** How long redemption takes, and under what conditions it can be gated or suspended, is not decided. It is one of the two things that determine whether Prime BTC can be unwound inside the liquidation window, and therefore whether it can carry a low liquidation rank at all. The other is whether the instrument can be sold — whether a secondary market exists for it with depth that executes under stress. Neither is established.

> **[OPEN — redemption SLA for Prime BTC and Prime Dollar not decided · owner: Product / Risk]**

**Fee split.** How the strategy's return is divided between the holder, the manager and the issuing party is not decided — and which entity that third party is has not been stated either. Until both are, no net yield to the holder can be described, because gross return minus an unknown split is unknown.

> **[OPEN — fee split on Prime BTC and Prime Dollar yield not decided · owner: Product / Finance]**

**Book segregation.** Whether the strategy book backing Prime BTC is segregated from other Velox activity, or from the activity of whichever entity issues the instrument, is not decided. This determines whether a loss elsewhere can reach this instrument, which is a counterparty question, not a strategy question.

> **[OPEN — whether the Prime BTC and Prime Dollar strategy book is segregated from other Arch or Velox activity is not decided · owner: Product / Legal]**

**Collateral haircut.** The haircut that would apply to Prime BTC if it is held as collateral is not decided. Without it you cannot compute the buying power the position would support.

> **[OPEN — collateral haircut for Prime BTC and Prime Dollar not decided · owner: Risk]**

**Liquidation rank.** The liquidation rank of Prime BTC is not decided, and cannot be set until the unwind route is. Deployed strategy positions are rank 1 and Bitcoin is rank 99, but a position cannot hold rank 1 unless it can be unwound inside the liquidation window by either route — sold on the secondary market, or redeemed with the strategy. The test is unwindability, not redeemability: a position with a deep secondary market can qualify despite long redemption terms, and a position with neither a workable redemption window nor executable depth cannot.

> **[OPEN — liquidation rank for Prime BTC and Prime Dollar not decided. Rank 1 requires unwindability inside the liquidation window by either route, and neither route is settled: the redemption SLA is undecided, and no executable secondary depth for either instrument has been established · owner: Risk]**

## How Prime BTC would be treated as collateral

This section describes how an asset of this shape *would* be treated once Arch Prime makes it eligible as collateral. It does not assert that Prime BTC is eligible today.

> **[OPEN — whether Prime BTC or Prime Dollar is the V1 whitelisted strategy, and whether either is collateral-eligible in V1 or arrives with heterogeneous collateral in V2, is not settled · owner: Product / Risk]**

The short version: a yield-bearing asset held as collateral behind a levered position would be that position's weakest point, not its strongest, and the arithmetic for why is on 3.5.

The reason in one line: in a levered account the binding constraint is not Bitcoin's price — it is the deployed position's NAV, and Prime BTC's NAV moves with the strategy as well as with Bitcoin. Capital efficiency in this account model comes almost entirely from counting the deployed position rather than from netting, and that is exactly the risk migration it implies: risk moves to the deployed leg, which is levered, which is why those positions are whitelisted, underwritten, and liquidated first.

Two of the parameters you would need to size that exposure — the haircut and the liquidation rank — are flagged above.

## What does not hold

- **Market-neutral is not risk-free, and no page in this set will say otherwise.** The specific bound is that the yield can be zero or negative.
- **This page states no yield rate, no historical return, and no execution-quality figure.** Not because they are omitted for space. Gross figures are unpublished, and the net figure cannot exist while the fee split is undecided.
- **Spot exposure to Bitcoin means the Bitcoin drawdown is fully carried.** The yield leg does not hedge the spot leg. Nothing here is a hedge.
- **Prime BTC is not archUSD.** It is not a wrapper on assets that already exist and it does not redeem at a fixed par. It is a position with a NAV.
- **Nothing on this page describes the structure the V1 strategy product is built as.** Those specifics are not settled and are not stated here.
