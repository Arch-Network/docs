This page explains what Prime Dollar is, how it differs from archUSD, where its yield comes from, and which of its mechanics have not yet been decided.

**"Prime Dollar" is a working title.** The name is not final and may change before launch. Read every reference on this page as pointing at the instrument, not at the label.

## What it is

Prime Dollar is a derivative asset that gives spot exposure to the dollar and also carries yield.

The instrument has the same two-part shape as Prime BTC, with one of the two parts behaving very differently:

**The spot leg.** Prime Dollar is dollar-denominated. It does not carry Bitcoin price risk.

**The yield leg.** Prime Dollar accrues a return from a trading strategy, and that strategy has its own profit and loss.

Because the spot leg is a dollar, effectively all of the variance in Prime Dollar comes from the yield leg. That is the opposite of the usual intuition about dollar-denominated instruments, and it is the reason the next section exists.

Which entity issues Prime Dollar, and which stands behind the strategy book, is not stated anywhere. Arch Network holds Bitcoin collateral and settles to Bitcoin; Arch Prime extends credit and lists strategies. Issuing this instrument is neither function, and this page names no responsible party for it.

> **[OPEN — no governing document states which entity issues Prime Dollar or holds the strategy book standing behind it. It is not established whether that party is Arch Network, Arch Prime, Velox, or a separate entity, which leaves the holder's counterparty unnamed · owner: Product / Legal]**

## Prime Dollar is not archUSD

These two are both dollar-denominated, both usable inside an Arch Prime account, and are not the same kind of thing at all. A reader who treats them as interchangeable will size a position wrong.

| | **archUSD** | **Prime Dollar** |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | A wrapper on dollars that already exist | A position in a trading strategy |
| What backs it | A 1:1 stablecoin reserve custodied at BitGo | The strategy book run by Velox |
| Yield | None. There is no yield leg | Yield from market-neutral, HFT quant-driven strategies |
| Can it lose value | Only through reserve failure or custodian failure | Yes. The strategy's NAV can fall, and the yield can be negative |
| What redemption returns | Not stated — see the archUSD page | The instrument's NAV at redemption, whatever that is |
| What it is for | Holding dollars that already exist, in on-chain form | Taking a dollar-denominated position that pays a return |
| Is the name settled | Yes | No — working title |

The one-line version: **archUSD wraps dollars that exist and pays nothing. Prime Dollar is a position in a strategy that pays yield and can lose value.**

A second distinction that matters more than it looks: archUSD is backed by a reserve of dollars that already exist, so the question at redemption is whether the reserve and the process function. Prime Dollar is a position in a strategy, so the question at redemption is what the strategy is worth that day. Those are different questions and they fail at different times. Neither instrument's redemption mechanics are stated — see the flags on both pages.

## Where the yield comes from

The yield on Prime Dollar comes from market-neutral, HFT quant-driven strategies managed by Velox Trading — the same source as Prime BTC.

> Arch has signed an LOI to acquire Velox, a seven-person quantitative trading firm, with close expected in [Q]. Velox's execution and risk capability is described throughout on the basis of its own historical books.

> **[OPEN — expected close quarter for the Velox transaction not stated · owner: Corp Dev]**

Velox brings the two prime brokerage functions that cannot be written in code: execution and a risk desk, with connectivity across roughly 60 venues. Arch Network, the infrastructure underneath the account, is a separate system: collateral remains an unspent transaction output on Bitcoin, locked under predefined Taproot spending conditions, and is never reissued on another chain.

Because Prime BTC and Prime Dollar draw on the same manager and the same strategy family, holding both is not diversification. It is one exposure held twice, with different spot legs attached.

## What "market-neutral" means, and what it does not

**What it means.** The strategy is not taking directional exposure. Its return is not intended to come from any asset going up. That describes the *source* of the return.

**What it does not mean.** Market-neutral does not mean risk-free. The strategy carries:

- **Execution risk.** The edge lives inside a spread. Slippage, latency, a mid-position venue outage, or a fill that does not arrive converts a modelled edge into a realised loss.
- **Model risk.** The relationship being traded can stop holding. Neutrality is a property of the model's assumptions; when they break, the book is directional and nobody chose that.
- **Counterparty risk.** Balances and positions sit with venues, brokers and custodians. A failure, a freeze, or a clawback is a loss the strategy did not trade into.
- **Venue risk.** Roughly 60 venues is both opportunity and surface area. Outages, halts, rule changes and settlement failures are venue events, and neutrality does not address them.
- **Operational risk.** Seven people, their systems and their keys. Error, key-person departure and control failure are live risks that neutrality does not diversify away.

**The bound, stated plainly.** The yield on Prime Dollar can go to zero and it can go negative. And because the spot leg is a dollar, a negative yield leg is not offset by anything — it comes straight out of the instrument's value. Holding a dollar-denominated instrument is not the same as holding dollars.

## What has not been decided

Each item below is separately undecided, with a separate consequence.

**Accrual method.** Exchange-rate accrual versus rebasing is not decided. It changes what the holder's balance looks like and how gains are measured and reported.

**Mint and redemption process.** How Prime Dollar is created and returned — by whom, against what, through what interface — is not decided.

> **[OPEN — mint and redemption process for Prime BTC and Prime Dollar not decided · owner: Product]**

**Redemption SLA.** How long redemption takes, and when it can be gated or suspended, is not decided. It is one of the two things that govern whether the instrument can be unwound inside the liquidation window, and therefore what liquidation rank it can hold. The other is whether it can be sold — whether a secondary market exists for it with depth that executes under stress. Neither is established.

> **[OPEN — redemption SLA for Prime BTC and Prime Dollar not decided · owner: Product / Risk]**

**Fee split.** How the return is divided between holder, manager and the issuing party is not decided — and which entity that third party is has not been stated either. No net yield to the holder can be described until both are.

> **[OPEN — fee split on Prime BTC and Prime Dollar yield not decided · owner: Product / Finance]**

**Book segregation.** Whether the strategy book is segregated from other Velox activity, or from the activity of whichever entity issues the instrument, is not decided. This determines whether losses elsewhere can reach this instrument.

> **[OPEN — whether the Prime BTC and Prime Dollar strategy book is segregated from other Arch or Velox activity is not decided · owner: Product / Legal]**

**Collateral haircut.** The haircut that would apply to Prime Dollar as collateral is not decided, so the buying power it would support cannot be computed.

> **[OPEN — collateral haircut for Prime BTC and Prime Dollar not decided · owner: Risk]**

**Liquidation rank.** The liquidation rank is not decided and cannot be set until the unwind route is. Deployed strategy positions are rank 1 and Bitcoin is rank 99, but nothing holds rank 1 unless it can be unwound inside the liquidation window by either route — sold on the secondary market, or redeemed with the strategy. The test is unwindability, not redeemability: a position with a deep secondary market can qualify despite long redemption terms, and a position with neither a workable redemption window nor executable depth cannot.

Rank 1 requires unwindability inside the liquidation window by either route, and neither route is settled: the redemption SLA is undecided, and no executable secondary depth for either instrument has been established · owner: Risk]**

## How Prime Dollar would be treated as collateral

This section describes how an asset of this shape *would* be treated once Arch Prime makes it eligible as collateral. It does not assert that Prime Dollar is eligible today.

> **[OPEN — whether Prime BTC or Prime Dollar is the V1 whitelisted strategy, and whether either is collateral-eligible in V1 or arrives with heterogeneous collateral in V2, is not settled · owner: Product / Risk]**

A dollar-denominated collateral asset looks stable on a price chart, and that appearance is the trap. Its price volatility input is low; its NAV can still fall, and neither of its ways out is established. Haircuts are a function of price volatility, enforcement certainty, and the unwind route — and the unwind route is the input most often forgotten. It has two forms that fail differently: redemption is slow but contractual, and secondary depth is fast but conditional and withdraws under stress. An instrument underwritten on depth alone is underwritten on the route most likely to be gone when it is needed.

In a levered account the binding constraint is not the collateral's headline price. It is the deployed position's NAV. The capital efficiency in this account model comes almost entirely from counting the deployed position rather than from netting, and the risk migrates with it: risk moves to the deployed leg, which is levered, which is why those positions are whitelisted, underwritten, and liquidated first. The arithmetic is on 3.5.

## What does not hold

- **Dollar-denominated is not dollar-stable.** Prime Dollar is a NAV, not a par claim. If you want a par claim, that is archUSD, and it pays nothing.
- **Market-neutral is not risk-free.** The specific bound: the yield can be zero or negative.
- **This page states no yield rate and no historical return.** Gross figures are unpublished, and a net figure cannot exist while the fee split is undecided.
- **Holding Prime BTC and Prime Dollar together is not diversification.** Same manager, same strategy family, one exposure.
- **The name is not final.** Any downstream material, integration, or contract referencing "Prime Dollar" should expect a rename.
- **Nothing here describes the structure the V1 strategy product is built as.** Those specifics are not settled and are not stated.
