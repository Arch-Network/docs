---
title: The margin account
---


This page describes the account object itself: what it holds, how its health factor is
computed, and how V1 margin is calculated. The account and its parameters are Arch Prime's;
the collateral the account is measured against is held and moved by Arch Network.

## The object

A margin account holds three things and derives one number:

- **An owner.** The account belongs to one party. There is no netting across accounts.
- **A set of positions.** Your posted Bitcoin, and any receipt tokens from strategies you
  have deployed into. Deployed positions live inside the account; they are not held by you
  and re-pledged. A receipt is a transferable instrument — that is what allows a position
  to be closed out by sale — but you never have possession of it. The enforcement property
  is possession, not transferability.
- **A set of debts.** The borrowings issued against the account.
- **A single health factor**, derived across all of the above. This is the number Arch
  Prime's account rules act on. Every action you take is accepted or rejected on the basis
  of what it does to this number.

## The arithmetic

Collateral value is the sum of each position's marked value after its haircut:

```
collateralValue = Σ (quantity × price × (1 − haircut))
```

Health factor is that value over everything you owe:

```
healthFactor = collateralValue / totalDebt
```

And liquidation is triggered on one condition:

```
liquidation is triggered when healthFactor < 1
```

Read the health factor as a ratio, not a percentage. At 2.0 your haircut collateral is
twice your debt. At 1.2 it is 20% above it. Below 1.0 the account is in liquidation.

## What the haircut does

The haircut is the discount applied to a position's mark before it counts as collateral.
Arch Prime sets it, per asset, as a function of three inputs:

```
haircut = f(price volatility, enforcement certainty, unwind route)
```

The unwind route is decisive, and it is the input most often forgotten. An asset that is
volatile but can be exited immediately can carry a smaller haircut than a stable asset that
takes a month to exit, because the haircut is not only a price buffer — it is a statement
about whether the position can be turned into repayment inside the window the liquidation
process has.

The route has two forms, and they fail differently. **Redemption is slow but contractual.**
**Secondary depth is fast but conditional, and it withdraws under stress.** A position
underwritten on depth alone is underwritten on the route most likely to be gone when it is
needed. See [4.4 Liquidity and unwind risk](../04-risks/4.4-liquidity-and-redemption-risk).

## V1 margin is additive

There is no correlation modelling in V1. Each position contributes its own haircut value
to the numerator; each debt contributes its face to the denominator. Nothing offsets
anything.

This is deliberate. It means the account's margin requirement is predictable and can be
computed by anyone reading the same inputs, and it means a modelling error in a
correlation assumption cannot silently inflate your buying power.

It also means the capital efficiency gain comes almost entirely from counting the deployed
position, not from netting. The deployed receipt is collateral, so deploying does not
consume your borrowing capacity the way withdrawing would. That is the whole of the gain.
And it carries its own consequence: risk moves to the deployed leg, which is levered,
which is why those positions are whitelisted, underwritten, and liquidated first.

## Limits

- **The account is not cross-margined.** Positions do not offset. A hedge in one leg does
  not reduce the requirement in another. True portfolio margin is V2.
- **No netting across accounts.** Two accounts with the same owner are two accounts.
- **The health factor is only as good as the marks.** It depends on the price of each
  position, and for a deployed strategy that price is a net asset value produced by a
  process outside Arch Network. That dependency is a risk in its own right and is covered
  in the risk section.
- **A health factor above 1.0 is not a buffer of any specific size.** What headroom
  you actually have depends on the deployed position's impairment behaviour, not on
  Bitcoin's price alone. See
  [Account health and liquidation](2.5-account-health-and-liquidation).
- **No liquidation-threshold number appears here.** The threshold figures circulating in
  internal material are inconsistent with the deployed market and are being reconciled.
