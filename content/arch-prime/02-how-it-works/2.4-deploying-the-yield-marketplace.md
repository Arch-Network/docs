title: Deploying — the yield marketplace

This page describes what happens when you put buying power to work: the single atomic step
that borrows and deploys, why you never hold the borrowed funds, and how you get out.

## The marketplace, and what you are actually trading

The reason to hold the account is a marketplace of different types of yield products Arch
Prime has listed, with leverage coming from looping those assets. Trading is not in scope
for V1. What you are trading is yield: which strategy, at what leverage, against which
terms of exit — the strategy's redemption terms, and whatever market exists for its
receipt.

Strategies are described in this documentation generically — by what they must satisfy to
be listed and how they behave inside your account — because the product description of the
V1 strategy itself is not settled.

## What a strategy must satisfy to be listed

A listed strategy is not a strategy someone published. It is one Arch Prime has whitelisted
and hand-underwritten, and it has to meet requirements that exist for the liquidation
engine's benefit, not the manager's:

- **It must be whitelisted by Arch Prime.** Deployment targets are enumerated. Buying power
  cannot be pointed at an arbitrary address.
- **It must produce a receipt the account can hold and mark.** The receipt is minted
  directly into the margin account and registered as a position. If it cannot be marked, it
  cannot be collateral.
- **It must carry a haircut Arch Prime has assigned**, set from price volatility,
  enforcement certainty, and the unwind route.
- **It must be unwindable inside the liquidation window if it is to be liquidated early.**
  A position cannot hold liquidation rank 1 unless it can be unwound inside the window, by
  either route — sold on the secondary market, or redeemed with the strategy. The test is
  unwindability, not redeemability. A position with a deep secondary market can qualify
  despite long redemption terms; a position with neither a workable redemption window nor
  executable depth cannot.
- **It must be sized against concentration limits** — with the standing caveat that V1 runs
  one whitelisted strategy, which means 100% concentration by construction. That is
  acceptable only because the strategy is hand-underwritten, and it is not a precedent.
  Per-strategy and per-manager caps arrive in phase two.

## The borrow-and-deploy bundle

Borrowing and deploying are not two transactions you perform in sequence. They are one
atomic bundle, executed on Arch Network, and it runs in this order:

1. **Validate.** The collateral in the account and the requested borrow are checked.
2. **Issue the debt.** The borrowing is created against the account.
3. **Deposit the proceeds.** The borrowed funds go into the whitelisted strategy.
4. **Mint the receipt directly into the margin account.** Not to you. To the account.
5. **Register the receipt as a position**, so it counts as collateral at its haircut.
6. **Recompute health and assert it.** The resulting health factor must satisfy the
   assertion in the bundle: a health factor at or above 1.

If any step fails — including the final health assertion — **the whole bundle reverts.**
There is no intermediate state to be stuck in, no partially-deployed position, and no debt
issued against a deposit that did not happen.

Leverage is built by repeating this shape: looping the deployed asset through further
borrow-and-deploy instructions until the target leverage is reached, with the health
assertion applied to the end state.

## Why you never hold the borrowed funds

The client never holds the borrowed funds, and never holds the receipt in their own wallet.
The receipt is minted into the margin account, and it stays there while debt is outstanding.

**The enforcement property is possession, not transferability.** The receipt *is* a
transferable instrument — that is what makes closeout by sale possible at all. What the
client does not have is custody of it. There is no step at which the borrower has
possession of transferable value.

This is enforcement by transaction shape, not by policy. It is not a rule that you agree
not to withdraw, monitored after the fact. The bundle has no step that pays out to you, so
there is no moment at which borrowed capital exists in a form you could move. A borrow with
no deployment attached to it simply is not an instruction that exists.

That property is what lets the account extend more buying power than a plain loan would:
the borrowed value never leaves the collateral perimeter. And it comes with the cost stated
everywhere in this set — risk moves to the deployed leg, which is levered, which is why
those positions are whitelisted, underwritten, and liquidated first.

## Getting out

There are three ways out. Two of them close the deployed position; the third repays around
it.

- **Sell the deployed position.** The receipt is a transferable instrument, and in many
  cases closeout, deleveraging and client exit are a swap of the receipt on a DEX rather
  than a redemption and burn. The proceeds of the sale retire the borrowing.
- **Redeem the deployed position with the strategy.** The position is redeemed on the
  strategy's terms, the receipt is burned, and the proceeds retire the borrowing.
- **Repay from external funds and withdraw your Bitcoin.** You bring the money in from
  outside, the debt is cleared, and Arch Network releases the collateral's spending
  conditions in a Bitcoin transaction.

In all three, the debt is retired before collateral is released, and **no path extracts
borrowed capital from the account.**

The two closing routes are not interchangeable, and the difference is the thing to read
before the yield number. **Redemption is slow but contractual** — it runs on the strategy's
stated terms, and those terms include whatever gates, notice periods and suspension rights
the documents provide for. **Sale is fast but conditional** — it depends on there being a
book to sell into at the moment you need one, and it realizes the market's price rather
than the strategy's NAV. Neither route is presented here as the normal one.

## Limits

- **Deployment is not diversification.** With one whitelisted strategy in V1, deploying is
  a single concentrated exposure regardless of position size.
- **Yield is not a floor.** A listed strategy's return can fall to zero or go negative, and
  its impairment reaches your equity before Bitcoin's price does.
- **Both unwind routes bind at the worst time.** A strategy that is redeemable in normal
  conditions but gated in stressed ones, and a receipt with visible depth in calm markets
  and none in a cascade, are the same problem reached by two paths. The haircut and rank
  were set on an assumption about one or both routes, and it is an assumption you should
  check.
- **A sale realizes market price, not NAV.** The account is marked at NAV; a receipt sold
  into the market fetches what the book will pay. See
  [4.3 Strategy and NAV risk](../04-risks/4.3-strategy-and-nav-risk).
- **The receipt is a position, not a claim you control.** It is transferable, but you never
  have possession of it: it is minted into the account, and while debt is outstanding it is
  subject to liquidation before your Bitcoin is.
- **You cannot borrow to withdraw.** Buying power exists only in deployable form.
