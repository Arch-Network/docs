This page answers two operational questions: when you can actually get access to build
against Arch Prime, and where the callable reference lives, since it is not in this
section.

## Access is staged, and the stage is set by capital

The thing to understand before you plan a timeline: committed lending dollars, not code,
gate the launch. Leverage capacity is a direct function of stablecoin depth, so the launch
is a capacity ladder, not a date.

That is a statement about the operating business, and it comes before anything about
roadmap. A builder who plans against a release date will be wrong in both directions —
early on the parts that depend on depth, late on the parts that do not.

**The ladder's shape**, stated as shape and gating logic rather than as a forecast:

| Rung | Depth |
|---|---|
| R0 — closed beta | ≤ $10M |
| R1 — first cohort | $25M |
| R2 — scale-up | $50M |
| R3 — open with caps | $100M |
| R4 — uncapped | $250M+ |

The served-collateral and account-count figures that accompany this ladder internally are
modelled, not committed, and are not published here or anywhere else in this set. Treat
the depth column as the gating variable, not as a schedule.

**Four gates open a rung**, and all four are conditions rather than milestones:

1. **Committed and papered depth.** Indications do not count.
2. **Security.** A second audit, formal verification of the liquidation path, and a funded
   bug bounty.
3. **Verification.** Readiness evidence with a named verifier.
4. **Disclosure.** Take rate defined, strategy product reconciled, and risk parameters
   correct in every public surface.

The fourth gate is the one that touches you directly. If your product restates an Arch
Prime risk parameter, your surface is part of "every public surface," and a parameter that
is wrong in your UI is a gate item.

**Rationing, while depth is scarce.** Rationing is by per-account borrow cap expressed as
a share of available depth. The recommendation is to start at 2% and step down as depth
grows; at a 2% cap, fifty accounts can sit at full cap simultaneously. Target utilisation
is 85% — the rate curve inflects at 90%, and 85% preserves withdrawal headroom.

For an integrator this means borrow capacity is a live, shared, moving quantity. A product
that assumes a user can always draw the amount your UI last computed will produce failures
that look like bugs and are not.

There is no minimum account size and no minimum ticket. A minimum would contradict the
reason Prime exists.

## Environments

Prime needs somewhere to build against that is not production, and the specifics of that
are not settled in this set.

> Engineering]**

> **[OPEN — the access process for integration partners at each rung of the capacity
> ladder is not specified: how a partner applies, what is required, and what is granted at
> R0 versus later rungs · owner: Product / Business Development]**

Two things you can plan around regardless. Arch Network is a Layer 1, an independent
chain, so your integration targets the network directly rather than an intermediary. And
Bitcoin settlement appears as an ordinary Taproot-signed transaction with no distinguishing
marker, so Bitcoin-side tooling you already run will see nothing exotic.

## Where the callable reference lives

**Not in section 7.** This section is the concept surface. It explains the model, the
transaction shapes, the state you read, and the integration patterns. It deliberately
stops before the interface.

The callable reference lives in the **Arch developer book**. That is where these belong,
and where they are maintained:

- API reference — endpoints, parameters, request and response shapes
- SDK reference, per language, with package names and versions
- CLI usage
- Error codes and their semantics
- Event delivery and webhooks
- Authentication, rate limits and pagination

Nothing in section 7 is an interface contract. If you find a name in these pages that
looks like a function, a field or an endpoint, it is illustrative of the model and is not
the thing you call.

> delivery · owner: Developer Relations]**

> this set; the list above is what a builder needs, not a statement of what has shipped ·

## What you supply, what Arch Prime and Arch Network supply, what is not yet available

**You supply:** your integration timeline, built against a rung rather than a date; correct
restatement of any Arch Prime risk parameter that appears in your product; and your own
handling of borrow capacity that can move underneath a user mid-flow.

**Arch Prime supplies:** the ladder and its gating logic, the rationing rule, the account
and liquidation mechanics documented in this section, and the callable reference in the
developer book.

**Arch Network supplies:** the chain your integration targets directly, and Bitcoin
settlement as an ordinary Taproot-signed transaction with no distinguishing marker.

**Not yet available:** named environments and their endpoints, the partner access process,
the confirmed inventory of callable surfaces for Prime V1, and the canonical location of
the developer book. All four are flagged above.

## What does not hold

- **A rung is not a date.** Depth gates it, and depth is committed and papered or it does
  not count.
- **The depth figures are not forecasts.** They are the ladder's gating variable. The
  modelled served-collateral and account-count figures behind them are not published.
- **Capacity is not per-integrator.** The per-account borrow cap is a share of available
  depth shared across all accounts, whatever product they arrived through.
- **This page does not grant access.** It describes what determines access. The process
  itself is flagged.
