This page states what Arch Prime does with your Bitcoin, in the order it happens.

## The mechanism

Arch Prime is the Bitcoin-native on-chain prime brokerage. It is a business, and it is
built on Arch Network — the chain that holds the Bitcoin collateral, authorizes any
movement of it, and settles to the Bitcoin network. Arch Prime extends the credit; Arch
Network clears and settles it. [Arch Network and Arch Prime](1.4-arch-network-and-arch-prime)
sets out the split in full, and this page assumes it.

You post native Bitcoin as collateral. Posting it generates a margin account. The margin
account carries buying power. You use that buying power to deploy into assets and yield
strategies Arch Prime has listed.

Four steps, in sequence:

**1 · You post native Bitcoin.** The coin stays an unspent transaction output on Bitcoin,
locked under predefined Taproot spending conditions. It is not wrapped, bridged, or
reissued on another chain. Nothing about the asset changes except the conditions under
which it can be spent.

**2 · The posting generates a margin account.** The account holds an owner, a set of
positions, and a set of debts, and derives a single health factor across all of them.
That health factor is the one number Arch Prime's account rules act on.

**3 · The account carries buying power.** Buying power is the borrowing the account's
collateral will support after haircuts. In V1 the borrowing runs through isolated pools,
and leverage comes from multi-instruction looping of the deployed asset on a per-strategy
basis.

**4 · You deploy.** Borrowing and deployment happen as one atomic bundle. The bundle
validates the collateral and the requested borrow, issues the debt, deposits the
proceeds into the strategy Arch Prime has whitelisted, mints the receipt directly into
your margin account, registers it as a position, recomputes health and asserts it — or
the entire bundle reverts and nothing happened.

## Why this can exist: enforcement without possession

Credit against Bitcoin has historically required the Bitcoin to move to whoever extends
the credit. The property Arch Network provides is different: enforcement without
possession. The client keeps custody, and the lender gets seizure rights that are capped,
automatic, and dependent on nobody's cooperation.

Those rights are enforced by the spending conditions on the coin itself and by Arch
Network's authorization layer, in which key shares are distributed in proportion to
stake weight, so no single operator — including Arch's own developers — can move
collateral unilaterally. The client retains custody; enforcement does not require
possession.

One boundary belongs in the same breath as the claim: enforcement determines that closeout
begins, not the price at which it completes. Rule-based enforcement fixes the trigger and
the sequence. It does not fix the market you are closed out into.

## What you get here that you do not get elsewhere

You get credit against native Bitcoin without transferring the Bitcoin to the party
extending the credit, and you get it at any size. There is no minimum account size and no
minimum ticket, because Arch Prime is not underwriting each client off its own balance
sheet — client financing is supplied by third-party liquidity providers, so the marginal
client costs nothing to onboard. A prime broker funding the book from its own balance
sheet cannot say that, which is why small self-custody accounts go unserved.

## The trade, stated plainly

You are taking on leverage, and leverage relocates your risk. Risk moves to the deployed
leg, which is levered, which is why those positions are whitelisted, underwritten, and
liquidated first.

Three consequences follow, and none of them are edge cases:

- In a levered account the binding constraint is not Bitcoin's price. It is the net asset
  value of the position you deployed into. See
  [Account health and liquidation](../02-how-it-works/2.5-account-health-and-liquidation)
  for the arithmetic.
- V1 runs one whitelisted strategy, which means 100% concentration by construction. That
  is acceptable only because the strategy is hand-underwritten, and it is not a precedent.
  Per-strategy and per-manager caps arrive in phase two.
- Yield is not a floor. A listed strategy's return can go to zero or below, and its
  impairment is what reaches your equity.

## What this is not

- It is not a trading product. Trading is out of scope for V1; what you are trading is
  yield.
- It does not net risk across your positions. V1 margin is additive, and true portfolio
  margining arrives in V2.
- It does not give you external venue connectivity, tokenized equities or securities-like
  collateral, correlation netting, or cross-account netting. All are out of scope for V1.
- It does not rehypothecate your collateral. Rehypothecation of any kind is out of scope
  for V1, and out of scope permanently unless explicitly reintroduced.
- "Trade anywhere" is not shipping. It is staged.
