## The job, in traditional markets

Hedge funds and high-net-worth individuals want access to many venues and products without
having to manage and rebalance liquidity across all of them. A prime broker is the party
that absorbs that work. It sits between the client and the market and provides four
things:

**Venue and product access.** One relationship instead of an account, a balance and a
margin requirement at every venue the client wants to touch.

**Execution.** Orders worked across venues by people and systems whose job is fill quality,
rather than by the client's own desk.

**Yield access.** A route into financing, lending and return-generating products the
client would otherwise have to source individually.

**Credit against the portfolio.** The function everything else hangs on. The broker
extends credit against assets the client already holds, which converts a static portfolio
into instant liquidity and buying power without the client selling anything.

Arch Prime does the same job, starting with Bitcoin collateral, on a vertically integrated
stack for scaling risk management and capital efficiency across real-world assets and
crypto assets. What makes that possible is an infrastructure fact rather than a commercial
one, and it belongs to Arch Network rather than to Arch Prime: collateral stays an unspent
transaction output on Bitcoin under predefined Taproot spending conditions, and the credit
is enforced against it without the collateral changing hands.

## Which functions are performed today, and by which entity

The functions divide cleanly, and the division is the useful thing to carry away.

### Arch Network: clearing and settlement

**Holding the collateral — live.** The coin stays a Bitcoin UTXO under predefined Taproot
spending conditions. The client retains custody; enforcement does not require possession.

**Authorization and settlement — live.** Any movement of collateral requires a threshold
signature Arch Network produces, and it reaches Bitcoin as an ordinary Taproot-signed
transaction. Offering credit against an asset the credit provider does not control is
something a custody-transfer arrangement cannot do by construction, which is why this leg
sits with the clearinghouse and not with the broker.

### Arch Prime: financing and counterparty underwriting

**Credit against the portfolio — live.** You post native Bitcoin, an account is generated,
and Arch Prime extends buying power against it. This is the function V1 is built around.

**Counterparty underwriting — live.** A lending protocol prices collateral; Arch Prime
prices a counterparty, and sets the haircuts, caps and liquidation ranks the account runs
under.

**Yield access — live.** A marketplace of yield products Arch Prime has listed is the
reason to hold the account at all. Leverage comes from looping those assets.

## What is staged

Everything in this section is an Arch Prime function that is not live.

**Venue access — staged.** External venue connectivity is out of scope for V1. "Trade
anywhere" is not shipping; it is staged and is presented as such everywhere it appears.

**Execution — staged, and it arrives through people.** Execution and a risk desk are the
two prime brokerage functions that cannot be written in code. They are being brought in
rather than built:

## The limits of the analogy

- A traditional prime broker finances clients off its own balance sheet. Arch Prime does
  not. Client financing is supplied by third-party liquidity providers, and Arch Prime
  earns a spread on flow it does not finance. That changes who bears the credit risk and
  who decides how much capacity exists.
- Because capacity comes from committed lending dollars rather than from Arch Prime's own
  balance sheet, growth is gated by depth. The launch is a capacity ladder, not a date.
- A traditional prime broker rehypothecates client assets. Arch Prime does not.
  Rehypothecation of any kind is out of scope for V1, and out of scope permanently unless
  explicitly reintroduced.
- Custody, financing and execution are not one party here. Custody is the client's,
  financing is the lenders', and clearing and enforcement are Arch Network's. Arch Prime
  is the broker in the middle, and it is not any of the other three.
