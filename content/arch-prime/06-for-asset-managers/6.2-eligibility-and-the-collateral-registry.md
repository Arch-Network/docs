---
title: Eligibility and the collateral registry
---


This page answers what a strategy has to satisfy before a Prime account can hold it, and
what the account actually holds once it does.

Whatever your strategy is off-chain, inside a Prime margin account it is a registry entry:
a small set of fields the risk engine reads every time it recomputes an account's health.
The fields are the strategy, as far as the system is concerned. If a property of your
strategy is not expressible in them, it is not a property the engine can act on.

## The registry entry

| Field | What it is | Who supplies it |
|---|---|---|
| Asset identifier | The identifier of the position asset that is minted into the margin account and registered as a position | You supply; Arch Prime records |
| Valuation source | How the engine prices the position: an oracle feed, a published NAV, or a fixed value | You supply the method and the feed; Arch Prime decides whether it is usable |
| Haircut | The discount applied to the position's value when computing collateral value | Arch Prime sets |
| Maximum account weight | The largest share of a single account's collateral value the position may represent | Arch Prime sets |
| Liquidation rank | The order in which the position is unwound during a forced closeout | Arch Prime sets, gated by whether the position can be unwound inside the window |
| Borrow eligibility | Whether the position counts toward buying power for new debt, or is held without supporting further borrowing | Arch Prime sets |
| Redemption SLA | The window inside which a redemption request is expected to settle in cash the engine can use to repay debt | You supply; Arch Prime relies on it |
| Secondary-market depth for the receipt* | What the market for the receipt will absorb, and how quickly, when the position is sold rather than redeemed | You supply what you know; Arch Prime assesses it |

\* An eligibility and haircut input rather than a settled field. It belongs in this table
because rank 1 now turns on it, and it is marked because whether it is recorded in the
registry has not been decided.

> **[OPEN — whether secondary-market depth for the receipt is recorded in the collateral registry at all is unspecified: there is no defined measure of depth, no stated observation source or cadence, and no rule for what happens to a registry entry when depth deteriorates · owner: Risk / Protocol Engineering]**

> **[OPEN — the collateral registry entry shape is not ratified: the maximum account weight and borrow-eligibility fields have no defined values and no owner, and are carried in the risk parameter reference as not defined · owner: Risk / Protocol Engineering]**

Those fields feed one calculation:

```
collateralValue = Σ (quantity × price × (1 − haircut))
healthFactor    = collateralValue / totalDebt
```

Liquidation is triggered when `healthFactor < 1`.

### What each field means for you

**Asset identifier.** The receipt is minted directly into the margin account and never
into the client's wallet. The enforcement property there is possession, not
transferability: the receipt is a transferable instrument, and the client simply never has
custody of it. Your transfer restrictions, lockups and whitelists have to be compatible
with a position that is held by an account, sold or redeemed during closeout, and moved by
a process the client does not initiate. A transfer restriction that would block a sale of
the receipt does not merely inconvenience the engine — it removes one of the two routes out
of the position.

**Valuation source.** The engine needs a price at the moment health is recomputed, not at
the next monthly close. An oracle feed is priced continuously. A published NAV is priced
at the cadence and by the method you commit to, and is only usable if Arch Prime can
verify it independently. A fixed value is only appropriate where the position has no valuation risk
to express, and it does not remove the redemption question.

**Haircut.** Not a negotiation. It is an output of the underwriting in
[6.3](./6.3-underwriting-and-risk-parameters). What you can influence is its inputs.

**Maximum account weight.** V1 runs one whitelisted strategy, which means 100%
concentration by construction. That is acceptable only because that strategy is
hand-underwritten, and it is not a precedent. Per-strategy and per-manager caps arrive in
phase two.

**Liquidation rank.** Deployed strategy positions are rank 1. Bitcoin is rank 99 and is
the last thing touched. Rank 1 is where a deployed position needs to be for the account
model to work, and it is conditional on the position being unwindable inside the
liquidation window by one route or the other — see the disqualifiers below.

**Borrow eligibility.** Whether your position supports new debt is what makes looping
possible, and looping is where a client's leverage comes from. It is also what makes your
NAV load-bearing for someone else's borrowing capacity, not only for their equity.

**Redemption SLA.** The engine treats this as a fact about the world. Where a forced
closeout runs by redemption, it is the assumption underneath the schedule. Treat it as a
covenant, not as a target.

**Secondary-market depth for the receipt.** The other way the position turns into cash. If
the receipt can be sold inside the window, the redemption SLA is not the only thing holding
up rank 1 — and a strategy with long redemption terms can still be eligible. The two are
not interchangeable, though, and the difference is what makes this a separate field:
redemption is an obligation you owe, while depth is a condition of a market neither of us
controls. It can be there in every observation and absent on the day it is needed.

## The hard disqualifiers

These are not scoring inputs. A strategy that trips one of them cannot be deployed into
with borrowed money in the form it is in.

**A reflexive yield source.** If the return depends on the system's own activity — on
deposits continuing to arrive, on leverage continuing to be taken, on the strategy's own
subscriptions financing the return to earlier subscribers — it is disqualifying under
leverage. Reflexive yield fails precisely when a levered book needs it to hold: the
liquidations that impair the strategy are the same liquidations that withdraw the flow the
yield depends on. Your yield source has to map to cash flows generated outside Arch Prime.

**No route out inside the liquidation window.** A position cannot hold rank 1 unless it can
be unwound inside the window, by either route — the receipt sold, or the position redeemed
with you. Long redemption terms alone are not disqualifying if the receipt has a market deep
enough to clear inside the window. Neither route working is disqualifying. There is no
haircut large enough to substitute, because a haircut changes what the position is worth,
not whether the money arrives in time.

**A NAV that cannot be independently verified.** A valuation the risk engine cannot check
is a valuation it cannot use. Self-reported marks that no third party reproduces do not
become usable by being conservative, audited annually, or accompanied by a track record.
The question is not whether the number is honest. It is whether the engine can act on it
at the moment it has to.

## What you supply, what Arch Prime supplies, what does not exist yet

**You supply:** the position asset and its transfer and redemption mechanics; the
valuation method and the feed or publication that carries it, in a form a third party can
reproduce; the redemption SLA; what you know about the secondary market for the receipt,
including how it behaved the last time conditions were bad; the yield source mapped to
external cash flows; and the disclosures in
[6.5](./6.5-reporting-and-disclosure-obligations).

**Arch Prime supplies:** the registry entry itself; the haircut, maximum account weight,
liquidation rank and borrow eligibility; the health arithmetic that reads them; and the
liquidation engine that acts on them.

**Arch Network supplies:** the execution environment those programs run in, and the
authorization and settlement of the Bitcoin leg when the walk reaches the collateral.

**Not yet available:** the parameter values, and the eligibility programme that would let
you self-assess before applying.

> **[OPEN — the haircut, maximum account weight, liquidation rank and borrow eligibility assigned to any specific strategy are not published; there is no readable registry a manager can consult before applying · owner: Risk]**

> **[OPEN — the independent verification standard for a published NAV is not defined: which third parties are accepted, at what cadence, and what happens to a registry entry when verification lapses · owner: Risk]**

## What does not hold

- The fields above describe what the account holds and what the engine reads. They are not
  a submission form, and there is no interface specification behind them today.
- Passing the disqualifiers is not eligibility. It is the precondition for underwriting.
- Max LTV is stated in this set only with its open-items flag, and no liquidation LTV
  figure is stated anywhere. Do not size a strategy against a threshold number taken from
  an older Arch document.
- Nothing in the registry constrains what your strategy does with the money. It constrains
  what Arch Prime can prove about the position while a client is levered into it.
