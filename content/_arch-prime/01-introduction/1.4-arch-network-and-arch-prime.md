title: Arch Network and Arch Prime

This page separates two things that are often run together: Arch Network is the chain, and
Arch Prime is a business built on it.

## Arch Network is the chain

Arch Network is the Bitcoin clearinghouse. Its purpose is to control the Bitcoin collateral
and to settle to the Bitcoin network, with no discretionary operator in the path.

The mechanism underneath that sentence is ordinary to state and unusual to have. Collateral
remains an unspent transaction output on Bitcoin, locked under predefined Taproot spending
conditions; it is never reissued anywhere else. Moving it requires an authorization the
network produces collectively, with key shares distributed in proportion to stake weight, so
no single operator — including Arch's own developers — can move collateral unilaterally.
What reaches the Bitcoin network at the end is an ordinary Taproot-signed transaction.

### The reference class, and where the analogy stops

The useful comparison is DTCC and comparable clearinghouses in traditional markets. A
clearinghouse holds the asset, stands between counterparties so that neither has to trust
the other directly, and makes settlement final. Arch Network performs that function for
Bitcoin collateral.

That is an analogy about function and nothing else. It is not a claim of equivalent legal
status, equivalent regulation, or an equivalent guarantee. A clearinghouse in traditional
markets is a supervised legal institution, and the protections its members enjoy — default
funds, member guarantees, a regulator standing behind the arrangement — come from that
status, not from the clearing function. None of those protections are imported here. What
carries over is the shape of the job: hold the asset, sit between the counterparties, make
settlement final.

The analogy also breaks in one direction worth stating: a clearinghouse is an operator that
exercises judgment. Arch Network is not. Enforcement is rule-based and runs without a
discretionary party in the path — which is the property that makes it useful, and also the
reason it cannot exercise the discretion a clearinghouse sometimes does.

## Arch Prime is the prime brokerage

Arch Prime is a business. It extends credit against Bitcoin collateral, underwrites the
counterparties it extends that credit to, lists the strategies a client can deploy into, and
earns a spread on flow it does not itself finance. It is built on Arch Network and it is the
network's anchor tenant.

It is not the network, and the network is not it.

## Why the order matters

Prime brokerages need two things that do not come free: a clearinghouse, and capital
velocity.

**Capital velocity** is the rate at which the same collateral does work. Collateral that
sits still supports one position. Collateral that can be pledged, borrowed against, and have
the resulting position itself counted as collateral supports considerably more. That reuse
is where a prime brokerage's economics come from, and it is also where prime brokerages fail
when the reuse is built on promises rather than on possession of the asset.

A clearinghouse is what keeps velocity bounded rather than reckless. When the party holding
the collateral is the same party that can enforce against it, and enforcement does not
depend on anyone's cooperation, credit can be extended against collateral without the
collateral having to move to the lender. Take that away and every increment of velocity is another
unsecured promise in a chain of them.

Two precisions, because velocity is the word under which bad things usually travel:

- Velocity here does not mean rehypothecation. Client collateral is not lent out or reused
  by Arch Prime. Rehypothecation of any kind is out of scope for V1, and out of scope
  permanently unless explicitly reintroduced.
- The capital efficiency comes almost entirely from counting the deployed position, not from
  netting risk across positions. Which means the risk moves: it migrates to the deployed
  leg, which is levered, which is why those positions are whitelisted, underwritten, and
  liquidated first.

The causal order follows from this and it never reverses. Arch Network built the clearing
infrastructure that a prime brokerage on Bitcoin requires. Arch Prime exists as a product
because that infrastructure exists underneath it. The business did not come first and then
commission a chain to justify itself.

## Who does what

Read each column downward. These are two lists placed side by side, not matched pairs.

| Arch Network does | Arch Prime does |
|---|---|
| **Holds the collateral.** The coin stays a Bitcoin UTXO under predefined Taproot spending conditions. | **Extends credit.** Buying power against the collateral in the margin account. |
| **Authorizes Bitcoin transactions.** Any movement of collateral requires a threshold signature the network produces; no operator can produce one alone. | **Underwrites the counterparty.** A lending protocol prices collateral; a prime broker prices a counterparty. |
| **Sequences and prioritizes transactions.** Ordering is decided at the chain, including the priority given to transactions that carry systemic risk. | **Sets account parameters.** Haircuts, borrow caps, liquidation rank, and what an account may hold. |
| **Settles to the Bitcoin network.** Final settlement is a standard Taproot-signed Bitcoin transaction with no distinguishing marker. | **Lists strategies.** Which yield products a client can deploy into, and on what terms they are eligible. |
| | **Earns the spread.** Client financing is supplied by third-party liquidity providers; Arch Prime earns a spread on flow it does not finance. |

If a sentence in these docs cannot tell you which column it belongs in, the sentence is
wrong, not ambiguous.

## Arch Network is open

Nothing stops another prime brokerage being built on Arch Network. Nothing stops an
application that is not a prime brokerage at all. Arch Prime is the anchor tenant and the
earliest application on the network — the reference implementation, not the privileged one.

This matters to two different readers.

If you are deciding whether to build on the network, it tells you that the clearing layer is
not a captive component of somebody else's product, and that the guarantees it makes are
made to applications generally.

If you are an Arch Prime client, it tells you the same thing from the other side. What the
network does with your collateral — hold it, authorize movement, settle — it does because
those are the network's rules, not because Arch Prime is the business asking. Enforcement is
non-discretionary and survives Arch Prime's insolvency. That is the whole point of the
separation.

> for blockspace or settlement, and how an application other than Arch Prime obtains
> collateral-authorization rights · owner: Protocol Engineering + Product]**

## What the separation does not mean

- **It does not remove market risk.** Neither entity sets the price at which a position is
  closed. Rule-based enforcement determines that closeout begins and in what sequence, never
  the price at which it completes.
- **It does not remove the validator assumption.** The principal risk in the network is the
  honest-majority assumption. Participants controlling more than the signing threshold
  could, if they colluded, authorize transactions the rules do not permit. This is not one
  risk among several.
- **It does not remove upgrade authority.** Authority to upgrade the programs is a real
  central point of failure and is disclosed as one.
- **It does not make the network's obligations contractual to you.** Openness is a property
  of how the network is built. It is not a forecast that other applications will appear, and
  it is not a commitment Arch Prime is in a position to make on the network's behalf.
- **It is not a claim about legal separation.** This page describes which entity performs
  which function. It does not describe corporate structure, and nothing here should be read
  as a statement about insolvency remoteness between them as legal entities.

## How these docs use the two names

Stated plainly, because you will need it on every page that follows:

- **Arch Network** means the chain. Use it when the actor is holding collateral, authorizing
  a Bitcoin transaction, sequencing transactions, or settling.
- **Arch Prime** means the business. Use it when the actor is extending credit, underwriting
  a counterparty, setting an account parameter, listing a strategy, or earning a spread.
- **Bare "Arch"** is not used to mean either. Where you see it in older material, the
  material predates this distinction and should be read with care.

The sentence that holds both: Arch Prime is a prime brokerage; it exists because Arch
Network built the clearing infrastructure a prime brokerage on Bitcoin requires.
