title: Why a purpose-built chain
---


This page answers the objection an informed reader raises immediately: a prime brokerage
does not obviously need its own chain, so why is there one.

## The objection, stated properly

Nothing stops someone launching a prime brokerage on-chain and offering a similar set of
services on infrastructure that already exists. Lending markets, vaults, swap venues and
stablecoins are all available as deployed protocols. A competent team could assemble a
product that looks like this one on a chain it does not control, and it would work.

That concession is not a rhetorical move. It is the accurate starting point, and the
argument only means something if it survives it. The question is not whether the product can
be built on a rented chain — a chain whose ordering and priorities are set by someone else,
for everyone. The question is what cannot be built underneath it.

## What a rented chain cannot supply: priority lanes for clearing risk

A chain that was not designed for clearing is unaware of transaction type. It sees
transactions and it sees fees. It does not see that one transaction is a liquidation on
which a loan book's recoverability depends and another carries no systemic risk at all.
Because it cannot see the difference, it cannot give the liquidation any additional
priority. Both compete for the same blockspace on the same terms.

Now put that in the only conditions that matter. Blockspace is most contested at peak
volatility: activity spikes, fee markets rise, inclusion becomes uncertain and ordering
becomes contested. Peak volatility is also the exact moment a loan book's solvency depends
on liquidations clearing promptly, because that is when accounts breach and when the gap
between detection and recovery is most expensive.

So the two conditions coincide. The moment the clearing transaction most needs to go through
is the moment it is least assured of going through, and the chain has no way to tell that it
should. Treating those transactions identically is not neutrality. It is a pricing decision:
the chain is charging the same for systemic risk as for none, at the moment systemic risk is
highest. A prime brokerage running on that infrastructure inherits the mispricing and pays
for it in the recoverability of its book.

A chain built for clearing can make ordering a property of the system rather than an outcome
of a fee auction. That is the capability at issue: priority lanes for transactions that
carry clearing risk.

## The second failure: fragmentation

The second thing a rented chain gives you is a product assembled from parts that do not
belong to each other. Lending is one protocol. Vaults are another. Swapping is another.
Stablecoin issuance is another. Each is separately governed, separately parameterized, and
separately upgraded, and a levered position spans all of them at once.

In calm markets that coordination holds. Prices are orderly, liquidity is available, each
component behaves the way its parameters say it will, and the seams do not show.

In adversarial and volatile regimes, when risk cascades, the coordination between separately
governed protocols is the thing that fails. Not usually any single component — each one
tends to do exactly what it was built to do. That is the problem. Each is optimizing for its
own solvency, its own liquidity, its own depositors. None of them is optimizing for the
solvency of a position that spans all four, and no one of them is even able to see that
position whole. The failure is in the joints.

## The scale at which both problems bind

Both problems are survivable at moderate size. Around $100 million of notional, a book can
absorb the ordering uncertainty and the coordination gaps — losses land, and they are
losses, not solvency events. Read that $100 million as an order of magnitude illustrating
roughly where the constraint starts to bind, not as a threshold anyone crosses on a
particular day.

The argument is about scale, and it is worth being exact about that. It is not a claim that
the alternatives do not work. They work. It is a claim that the properties that make them
workable at that size stop being adequate as the book scales into the tens of billions —
which is what real global-scale capital markets for Bitcoin means. At that size, ordering
guarantees for risk-bearing transactions and a stack that does not have to negotiate with
itself become necessary, if not required.

## What this chain carries instead

Arch Network was designed against that requirement set rather than adapted to it. It carries
explicit optimizations for scaling lending: risk management, liquidation fidelity, risk
monitoring, and capital efficiency. Those are precisely the requirements of a Bitcoin prime
brokerage scaling lending from a risk perspective — which is unsurprising, since they were
the specification and not a discovery made afterwards.

## The posture difference

This is the practical difference and it is the point of the page.

Other lending protocols manage risk with conservatism. Wider parameters, larger buffers,
less credit extended per unit of collateral. That is a rational response to the position
they are in: they cannot control what happens between the moment a breach is detected and
the moment recovery completes. They cannot guarantee their liquidation gets sequenced ahead
of noise, and they cannot make four separately governed protocols cooperate in a cascade. So
they hold back capital against the interval they do not control.

Arch Prime manages risk proactively instead: multiple lines of defence, and a vertically
integrated stack whose parts work in harmony rather than negotiating with each other under
stress.

Here is what that means for a client, because it is easy to read as an architecture
preference rather than a term of business. Conservatism is not free, and it is not the
protocol that pays for it. The client pays, in credit they do not get — in the haircut that
is wider than the asset's behaviour warrants, and in the leverage that is capped below what
the collateral would support if the interval between detection and recovery were controlled.
A buffer sized for an uncontrolled interval is capital sitting idle in the client's account
to insure the protocol against something the protocol cannot govern.

Controlling that interval is what makes it possible to extend credit against the risk that
is actually there rather than against the risk plus the uncertainty. That is the entire
return on building the layer underneath.

## What this does not buy

Being honest about the ceiling matters more here than anywhere else on this page, because
everything above is an argument about infrastructure and infrastructure claims overreach
easily.

- **It does not remove market risk.** Bitcoin can fall, a deployed strategy can be impaired,
  and both can happen faster than any ordering guarantee helps with.
- **It does not guarantee a recovery price.** Rule-based enforcement is a claim about the
  certainty and timing of the recovery process, never about the price at which recovery
  occurs. Priority in sequencing decides that closeout begins and in what order. It does not
  decide what the market pays.
- **It does not make the validator assumption go away.** The principal risk is the validator
  honest-majority assumption: participants controlling more than the signing threshold could,
  if they colluded, authorize transactions the rules do not permit. Building the chain rather
  than renting one changes which set of participants you are trusting. It does not remove the
  trust. Upgrade authority over the programs is a separate and equally real central point of
  failure.
- **It substitutes one kind of risk for another.** A mature chain has years of adversarial
  operation behind it, a large validator set, and implementation bugs that have already been
  found by someone else. A newer chain does not. In exchange for ordering guarantees and an
  integrated stack, this set of clients takes on a newer implementation's defect risk and a
  newer network's adoption and liquidity risk. That is a real trade and it is being made
  deliberately, not avoided.
- **It does not make the coordination problem theoretical.** V1 runs one whitelisted
  strategy, which means 100% concentration by construction. Integration removes negotiation
  between components; it does not remove concentration.
