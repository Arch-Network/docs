title: Counterparty risk

This page lists every party a client of Arch Prime depends on, and states what each of them
can do to that client.

The account's design removes several counterparties that a client would otherwise have.
Collateral remains an unspent transaction output on Bitcoin, locked under predefined
Taproot spending conditions, and is never reissued on another chain. The client never
holds the borrowed funds, never holds the receipt in their own wallet, and there is no path
that extracts borrowed capital from the account — the enforcement property is possession,
not transferability, and the receipt remains a transferable instrument the client does not
have custody of. Rehypothecation of any kind is out of scope for V1, and out of scope
permanently unless explicitly reintroduced.

What follows is who is left.

## The strategy manager

**What they can do to you.** The manager has discretion over the deployed assets inside
the strategy's mandate. They can impair NAV through model failure, execution error,
position concentration, deviation from the mandate, or fraud. They can strike a NAV that
flatters the position. They can gate, queue or suspend redemptions, which closes one of
the two routes out and leaves the account dependent on there being a bid for the receipt
instead. They can wind the strategy down on their own timetable.

**What constrains them.** Whitelisting and hand underwriting before listing. Listing
conditions, including unwindability inside the liquidation window by sale or by redemption.
The haircut applied to the position. Reporting and disclosure obligations agreed at listing.

**What does not constrain them.** Anything Arch Network enforces. Enforcement operates on
the receipt registered in the margin account and on the Bitcoin collateral. It does not
operate on the manager's book, the manager's positions, or the manager's decisions. Once
assets are inside the strategy, the client holds a claim whose value is determined by
someone else's conduct.

## The stablecoin issuers behind archUSD reserves

archUSD is a 1:1 stablecoin-backed dollar with permissionless mint. It is not a CDP. It
wraps dollars that already arrived; it does not manufacture lending capacity.

That design removes one class of risk and creates another. There is no collateral ratio to
break and no liquidation cascade inside archUSD itself. What there is instead is a direct,
undiluted dependency on the issuers of the stablecoins held in reserve.

**What they can do to you.** Depeg, through a reserve shortfall or a loss of confidence.
Suspend redemption, which severs the mechanism that holds the peg. Freeze or blacklist
addresses, where the issuer's terms provide for it, which no on-chain enforcement
overrides. Change reserve composition. Fail.

**How it reaches your account.** archUSD holds its value only because the reserves hold
theirs. Wherever archUSD is used — as the borrowed asset, as the deployed asset, or as a
unit of account inside the account — a break in a reserve stablecoin passes straight
through, because there is no buffer in the design to absorb it. Permissionless mint means
the quality of the reserve set is a function of what is accepted into it.

## BitGo, as custodian of those reserves

The archUSD reserves are custodied at BitGo.

**What they can do to you.** Custodian failure of any kind reaches the reserves: insolvency,
operational failure, key compromise, legal process against assets held, or a jurisdictional
action. A single named custodian for the reserve holdings is a concentration, and it should
be read as one.

**Scope, stated precisely because it is easy to misread.** Your Bitcoin is not at BitGo.
Client collateral remains an unspent output on Bitcoin under Taproot spending conditions.
BitGo custodies the dollar reserves behind archUSD. These are two different assets with two
different failure modes, and a failure at the custodian reaches the second, not the first.

**What does not constrain it.** Arch Network's enforcement perimeter does not extend to a
custodian's balance sheet, its controls, or its regulators. Segregation and bankruptcy
treatment of the reserves are matters of BitGo's own arrangements.

## Arch Prime, as a business

Arch Prime is your counterparty. It opens the account, prices you as a borrower, and sets
the terms your position is measured against. That makes it a counterparty in the ordinary
sense, and it can fail in the ordinary ways a business fails.

**What Arch Prime can do to you.**

- **Whitelisting authority.** Arch Prime decides which strategy is listed. In V1 that single
  decision is 100% of every client's deployed exposure.
- **Parameter authority.** Haircuts, per-account caps, liquidation ranks and max LTV are set
  by Arch Prime, and changes to them change the risk of positions already open.
- **Underwriting discretion.** Which counterparties are onboarded, and on what terms, is a
  judgement rather than a rule. A judgement made badly reaches you through the parameters
  and through the quality of the manager sitting behind your deployed position.
- **Operational dependency.** Front ends and account state presentation are Arch Prime's. If
  they are wrong or unavailable, you cannot see the account or act on it, whatever the
  underlying state is.
- **Business failure or change of control.** Arch Prime can become insolvent, be acquired,
  or change who runs it. Each of those changes who exercises the authorities above.

**What is genuinely outside Arch Prime's reach.** Enforcement is non-discretionary and
survives Arch Prime's insolvency. Arch Prime does not fund client financing; third-party
liquidity providers do, so Arch Prime's balance sheet is not what stands behind an open
position. Arch Prime's failure as a business is not the same event as a failure in the
enforcement path, and the collateral rules do not require Arch Prime to be solvent in order
to run.

That is a real property and it is narrower than it sounds. It says a dead Arch Prime cannot
stop enforcement. It says nothing about what a living Arch Prime can do with the authorities
listed above.

## Arch Network, as infrastructure

Arch Network is a different exposure and it fails differently. It is not a party you
contracted with, it does not underwrite you, and no decision Arch Prime makes bounds it.

**What Arch Network can do to you.**

- **Capture above the signing threshold.** If participants controlling more than the signing
  threshold collude, they can authorize transactions the rules do not permit. That is the
  principal risk in the system, not one risk among several.
- **Halt.** Below the participation threshold, authorization stops — which stops liquidations
  and withdrawals as well as unauthorized movement, while the market keeps moving.
- **Reorganization at the Bitcoin layer.** Settlement is a Bitcoin transaction, and a
  reorganization can alter recently confirmed state, including state a liquidation or a
  position opening was computed against. See
  [4.8](./4.8-settlement-and-reorganization-risk).
- **Upgrade authority over the network's programs.** This is a real central point of failure.
  Whoever holds it can change the programs that define the collateral rules, the health
  computation, the liquidation ranks, the haircuts, and the conditions under which collateral
  moves. That authority sits above the account rules and is not constrained by them. Who
  holds it is not specified. See [4.7](./4.7-protocol-validator-and-upgrade-risk).

Each of these is covered in full in [4.7](./4.7-protocol-validator-and-upgrade-risk).

**Why the two are listed separately.** They have different owners, different failure modes
and different remedies, and diligence on one tells you nothing about the other. Arch Prime
could be run well and Arch Network captured. Arch Network could run exactly as specified
while Arch Prime sets a haircut that leaves your buffer thinner than you believed. You are
exposed to both at once, and there is no arrangement described in this set under which one
covers for the other.

## The lenders

Third-party liquidity providers finance the book. They can stop supplying, and they can
withdraw as capacity permits.

**What that does to you.** Your ability to open, maintain or roll a levered position
depends on capital that is not committed to you. Depth contracts as suppliers leave, and
contracting depth reduces the per-account borrow capacity available. A borrower whose
position is sound can still find that the leverage it depends on is being withdrawn from
the system around it. See [4.4](./4.4-liquidity-and-redemption-risk).

## Concentration in V1

V1 runs one whitelisted strategy. That is 100% concentration by construction.

It is acceptable only because the strategy is hand-underwritten, and it is not a precedent.
Per-strategy and per-manager caps arrive in phase two.

Stated as a client would need to hear it: in V1 there is no diversification of manager
risk, no diversification of strategy risk, and no cap that limits your exposure to a single
manager's conduct, because there is only one. Every failure mode in the strategy-manager
section above applies to every deployed dollar in the system simultaneously.

## The enforcement perimeter does not extend to external venues

For any strategy that routes assets to a venue outside Arch Network's enforcement perimeter,
the enforcement property does not extend there.

The mechanism is worth being exact about. Taproot spending conditions bind an output on
Bitcoin. The programs in the ArchVM enforce the account's rules on the receipt registered
in the margin account. Neither reaches an asset sitting at a third-party venue. Once assets
leave the perimeter, what the client holds is a claim, and the value of that claim depends
on that venue's solvency, its internal controls, its treatment of client assets, and its
willingness to honour a redemption request during a period of stress.

The property Arch Network provides is that seizure rights over the *collateral* are capped,
automatic and dependent on nobody's cooperation. It is not a property of assets held
elsewhere, and no amount of enforcement quality at the Arch Network layer changes what a
venue does with assets in its possession. Nor does Arch Prime's underwriting of the strategy
reach the venue: underwriting is a judgement made before listing, not a control that
operates on someone else's balance sheet.

## Who bears it

The client bears every counterparty failure on this page down to zero equity. Below that,
shortfall is a credit loss to the third-party liquidity providers who financed the
position. Arch Prime bears reputational and operational consequences and, in V1, bears the
underwriting judgement — but not the financing. Arch Network bears none of it: it is not a
party to the credit, and a failure in it lands on the client and the lenders directly.

## What reduces it

- Hand underwriting of the single whitelisted strategy, and the listing conditions that
  come with it.
- Haircuts, which price counterparty and unwind uncertainty into the collateral value.
- Rank ordering, which reaches the deployed leg before the client's Bitcoin.
- The transaction shape, which prevents borrowed funds from reaching the client or any
  venue other than the whitelisted strategy.
- Collateral remaining on Bitcoin under Taproot conditions, which keeps the largest single
  asset out of any custodian's hands.
- Per-strategy and per-manager caps, which arrive in phase two and do not exist now.

## What does not reduce it

- **Self-custody of the Bitcoin.** It protects the Bitcoin leg. It does nothing about the
  manager, the issuers, the custodian, the venues, Arch Prime, or Arch Network — and against
  a colluding threshold it does not protect the Bitcoin leg either, because the spending
  condition is what is being satisfied.
- **Haircuts.** A haircut prices a default. It does not prevent one, and it is sized for
  ordinary variation rather than for a counterparty failing outright.
- **Diversification.** There is none in V1.
- **Enforcement quality.** Rule-based enforcement determines that closeout begins, not the
  price at which it completes, and not whether the counterparty holding the assets is
  still there.
- **Arch Prime's insolvency-resistance.** It protects against one Arch Prime failure mode
  and none of the others on this page, and it says nothing at all about Arch Network.

## The residual

A single manager failure, a break at one reserve stablecoin issuer, a custodian failure, or
an exercise of upgrade authority over Arch Network's programs can each cost a V1 client
their full equity. None of them is diversified against, one of them is the party under the
acquisition LOI described above, and the custody model that would tell you which venues are
actually involved has not been reconciled.
