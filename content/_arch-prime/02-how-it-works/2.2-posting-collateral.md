title: Posting collateral
---


This page describes what happens to your Bitcoin when you post it, what stays on Bitcoin,
what Arch Network maintains, and who can and cannot move the coin afterwards. Almost
everything here is Arch Network's job: it is the clearinghouse that holds the collateral
and authorizes any movement of it. Arch Prime appears only where the credit relationship
is the subject.

## What posting actually does

Posting collateral into an Arch Prime margin account commits an existing unspent
transaction output to a set of spending conditions. It does not send your Bitcoin
anywhere.

The output remains an unspent transaction output on Bitcoin, locked under predefined
Taproot spending conditions. It is never reissued on another chain. There is no wrapped
representation, no bridge custodian, and no synthetic claim standing in for the coin. The
asset backing your account is the coin, at its own address, on Bitcoin.

What changes is the condition under which it can be spent. Before, the condition was your
signature. After, the condition is the spending policy the account was created under —
which admits your withdrawal when the account's health permits it, and admits enforcement
when it does not.

## What stays on Bitcoin, and what Arch Network maintains

**On Bitcoin:** the coin, the spending conditions, and every transaction that moves it.
Settlement appears on Bitcoin as an ordinary Taproot-signed transaction with no
distinguishing marker, because the signing scheme produces standard Schnorr signatures.
Nothing identifies the output as collateral to an outside observer.

**On Arch Network:** the account state. Arch Network is a Layer 1, an independent chain
whose programs run in the ArchVM, an eBPF-based environment written against in Rust. The
ArchVM indexes Bitcoin's chain and mempool, so a program can reference live Bitcoin state —
the existence of your output, its confirmations, its spend status — rather than trusting a
relayer's report of it.

The separation between these two matters more than any other detail on this page:
**agreement on Arch Network application state does not by itself move BTC.**
Authorization of the Bitcoin transaction is a separate step. Conflating the two is the
most common misreading of the architecture. Your account can be updated, your health
factor recomputed, your liquidation decided — and none of that has touched your coin
until a Bitcoin transaction is separately authorized and broadcast.

## Who can move the collateral

**You can**, subject to the account's health as Arch Prime's parameters define it.
Withdrawal is permitted when the resulting health factor is still at or above 1. A
withdrawal whose end state would leave the health factor below 1 is refused before Arch
Network is ever asked to authorize a Bitcoin transaction for it.

**Enforcement can**, under the conditions the account was created under, and only in the
sequence liquidation defines. Bitcoin is the last thing touched. Arch Prime's rules decide
that a closeout begins and in what order; Arch Network authorizes and settles the Bitcoin
transaction that results.

**Arch Network cannot, unilaterally.** Signing authority over the collateral is
distributed with FROST threshold Schnorr signatures, with ROAST for robustness when
signers are unavailable or adversarial. Key shares are distributed in proportion to
stake weight across validators running delegated proof of stake. No single operator —
including Arch's own developers — can move collateral unilaterally.

**No individual validator can.** A single share signs nothing. Only a threshold set does.

FROST and ROAST are published constructions from the peer-reviewed literature; Arch
Network did not invent them. Arch Network's contribution is their operation as the live
authorization layer of a system holding native Bitcoin collateral. The construction is
described in the Arch technical whitepaper, which is authoritative on it.

## Limits

- **The honest-majority assumption is the load-bearing one.** If participants controlling
  more than the signing threshold collude, they can authorize transactions the rules do not
  permit. This is not one risk among several — it is the principal risk of holding
  collateral under this design.
- **Upgrade authority over the programs is a real central point of failure** and is
  disclosed as such. The spending conditions are enforced by programs, and the party that
  can change those programs is a party you are exposed to.
- **A pre-confirmation is not Bitcoin finality.** A transaction can leave the mempool
  before confirmation, and a Bitcoin reorganization can alter recently confirmed state.
  See [Settlement on Bitcoin](2.6-settlement-on-bitcoin).
- **Posting is a commitment, not a deposit you can reverse at will.** While the collateral
  supports debt extended by Arch Prime, your ability to withdraw is a function of account
  health, not of your intent.
- **Enforcement determines that closeout begins, not the price at which it completes.**
