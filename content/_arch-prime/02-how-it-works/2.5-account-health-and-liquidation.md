title: Account health and liquidation
---


This page describes what the health factor measures, what happens as it falls, and the
order in which positions are unwound when it reaches the trigger.

Liquidation is the one process in this documentation that runs across both entities. The
decision and the ordering are Arch Prime's; the authorization and settlement of the Bitcoin
transaction that results are Arch Network's. The seam between them is marked below rather
than smoothed over, because knowing where it falls tells you which guarantees you have.

## The one number

```
collateralValue = Σ (quantity × price × (1 − haircut))
healthFactor    = collateralValue / totalDebt
liquidation is triggered when healthFactor < 1
```

The haircuts in the first line and the trigger in the third are Arch Prime's parameters.
Health is recomputed as marks move. It is not a daily snapshot and it is not something you
opt into monitoring.

## What happens as health degrades

Degradation is gradual until the trigger, and then it is not.

**While health is comfortably above 1**, the account behaves normally: you can deploy,
borrow further, or withdraw collateral, subject to the caps.

**As health falls toward 1**, actions start being refused rather than warned about. Any
instruction whose end state would leave the health factor below 1 reverts — the bundle
checks the resulting health and will not commit to a state that breaches it. Which
instructions become unavailable first is not specified.

> **[OPEN — whether a maintenance level above a health factor of 1 governs when actions are
> refused, and what that level is, is not specified. The only level in the governing
> documents is the liquidation trigger at a health factor below 1 · owner: Risk]**

**Below the trigger**, liquidation begins. It is rule-based and non-discretionary: nobody
at Arch Prime decides whether your account is liquidated, and nobody can decide not to.

You restore health the same way you would anywhere: repay debt, post more collateral, or
unwind part of the deployed position — by selling the receipt or by redeeming it with the
strategy. All three raise the ratio; the third is the one the liquidation engine will do
for you if you do not.

> **[OPEN — liquidation threshold / LLTV is not stated in this set, and no health colour
> bands are published, because every band in the corpus is arithmetically derived from a
> threshold figure that is inconsistent with the deployed market · owner: Risk]**

## Liquidation order: Bitcoin last

Liquidation walks positions by liquidation rank ascending until health is restored. The
ranks are Arch Prime's account parameters.

| Rank | Position type |
|---|---|
| 1 | Deployed strategy positions |
| 99 | Bitcoin |

Deployed strategy positions are rank 1. Bitcoin is rank 99. **Bitcoin is the last thing
touched**, and only if unwinding everything ahead of it did not restore health.

Those two are the only ranks the governing documents define. The gap in the numbering
leaves room for further ranks; none is specified, and no intermediate tier exists today.

The ordering is not a courtesy. It is the direct consequence of where the risk sits: risk
moves to the deployed leg, which is levered, which is why those positions are whitelisted,
underwritten, and liquidated first.

Rank is constrained by unwindability, not by preference. A position cannot hold rank 1
unless it can be unwound inside the liquidation window, **by either route** — sold on the
secondary market, or redeemed with the strategy. The test is unwindability, not
redeemability: a position with a deep secondary market can qualify despite long redemption
terms, and a position with neither a workable redemption window nor executable depth
cannot. If it cannot be turned into repayment inside the window by either route, ranking it
early would mean starting a process that cannot finish.

Which route a given closeout takes is not settled.

> **[OPEN — which unwind route is primary (sale of the receipt or redemption with the strategy), who selects it at closeout, and whether it varies by strategy, are not settled. Both routes are described here; neither is presented as the normal path · owner: Product / Risk]**

> **[OPEN — the duration of the liquidation window, the close factor per liquidation step,
> and the liquidation penalty or incentive paid to liquidators are not specified in any
> governing document · owner: Risk / Protocol Engineering]**

## Where the seam falls: Arch Prime decides, Arch Network settles

A liquidation is two things happening in sequence, performed by two different parties, and
the reader who keeps them apart understands what is actually guaranteed.

**Arch Prime's side: the decision and the order.** The haircuts that produce
`collateralValue`, the trigger at a health factor below 1, the rank each position holds,
and the requirement that a rank-1 position be unwindable inside the liquidation window are
all Arch Prime's account rules. When an account breaches, those rules determine that a
closeout begins and which position is reached for first. Nothing in this list is a
judgement made at the moment of stress; it is a parameter set beforehand.

**Arch Network's side: the authorization and the settlement.** The walk can reach the
client's Bitcoin, and Bitcoin cannot be moved by a decision. It moves only when Arch
Network produces a threshold signature over a Bitcoin transaction spending that output, and
only when Bitcoin confirms it. No operator — Arch Prime included — can produce that
signature alone.

**Why the seam is worth seeing.** Agreement on Arch Network application state does not by
itself move BTC; authorization of the Bitcoin transaction is a separate step, and
conflating the two is the most common misreading of the architecture. It is also what makes
the enforcement claim meaningful in the other direction: because the authorization does not
belong to Arch Prime, Arch Prime cannot decline to liquidate a favoured account, and it
cannot reach the collateral of an account that is healthy. The rules that bind you are the
same rules that bind the broker.

The seam sets the limits of the guarantee too. Arch Prime's rules fix the trigger and the
sequence; Arch Network's authorization fixes that the resulting Bitcoin transaction settles
without anyone's cooperation. Neither fixes the price, and the interval between the two is
market exposure.

## What actually wipes you out

In a levered account the binding constraint is not Bitcoin's price. It is the deployed
position's net asset value.

For an account with equity `E` and deployed position `V`, the impairment that wipes equity
is:

```
impairment that wipes equity = E / V
```

The more you deploy against the same equity, the smaller the NAV move that reaches you.
This is arithmetic, and it holds regardless of what the strategy is.

### Illustrative worked case

*Illustrative arithmetic only. These are not product parameters and not a representation of
any account, price, or strategy.*

| Input | Value |
|---|---|
| Collateral | 1 BTC at $100,000 |
| Deployed position | $500,000 |
| Debt | $500,000 |
| Equity `E` | $100,000 |
| `E / V` | $100,000 / $500,000 = 20% |

Two moves, both large, with opposite outcomes:

- **A 20% impairment in the deployed strategy wipes the client**, at full Bitcoin value.
  Bitcoin never moved. The strategy's NAV did.
- **A 100% Bitcoin drawdown leaves the account whole**, because the deployed position
  still covers the debt.

The lesson to take from it is not that Bitcoin risk is unimportant. It is that in a levered
account, the leg you are watching is usually not the leg that ends you.

## The scope boundary on enforcement

Enforcement determines that closeout begins, not the price at which it completes.

What Arch Prime's rules and Arch Network's authorization fix between them is the trigger,
the sequence, and the fact that the process runs without anyone's permission or
cooperation. What they do not fix is execution: the price
achieved when a position is unwound, the depth available to a sale at that moment, the
redemption behaviour of a strategy under stress, or whether the proceeds cover the debt in
full. Rule-based enforcement is a statement about certainty and timing of the recovery
process, never about the price at which recovery occurs. It does not create a buyer, and it
does not open a redemption window that the strategy has gated.

## Limits

- **Liquidation can leave a shortfall.** Closing out is not a promise that debt is fully
  repaid from proceeds.
- **The health factor depends on marks you do not control**, including a strategy NAV
  produced by a process outside Arch Network. A stale or wrong mark produces a wrong health
  factor in either direction.
- **There is no grace period documented here**, because none is specified. Do not assume
  one exists.
- **Additive margin means no offsets during stress.** A position that would hedge your
  exposure does not reduce the requirement that is liquidating you.
- **Deployed positions are unwound before Bitcoin, which means your yield exposure is what
  gets sold at the worst moment** — by design, because it is the levered leg.
- **If the unwind is a sale, the account realizes market price, not NAV.** The health
  factor is computed from the NAV mark; the proceeds are whatever the book pays. The two
  are not the same number, and they diverge most under stress. See
  [4.3 Strategy and NAV risk](../04-risks/4.3-strategy-and-nav-risk).
