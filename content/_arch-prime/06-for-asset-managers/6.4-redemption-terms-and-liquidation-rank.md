title: Unwind terms and liquidation rank
---


This page answers what your unwind terms do inside someone else's margin account, and why
Arch Prime underwrites them harder than anything else about your strategy.

The rule, stated once:

> A position cannot hold liquidation rank 1 unless it can be unwound inside the liquidation
> window. There are two routes — the receipt is sold, or the position is redeemed with you —
> and either one qualifies. A position with neither a workable redemption window nor
> executable depth in the receipt cannot be rank 1.

Deployed strategy positions are rank 1. If your strategy cannot be rank 1, it cannot be the
position a levered account holds. That is the whole of the eligibility question, compressed.

Which route a given closeout actually takes is not settled.

> **[OPEN — which unwind route is primary (sale of the receipt or redemption with the
> strategy), who selects it at closeout, and whether it varies by strategy, are not settled.
> Both routes are described here; neither is presented as the normal path · owner: Product /
> Risk]**

Read the rest of this page on the assumption that either route may be the one that runs.

## What rank means mechanically

Liquidation is triggered when an account's health factor falls below 1. The engine then
walks positions by liquidation rank ascending until health is restored. Deployed strategy
positions are rank 1. Bitcoin is rank 99. Bitcoin is the last thing touched.

The ordering is not a preference. It is the design: the client posted Bitcoin because they
want to keep it, and the deployed leg is the levered leg, which is why those positions are
whitelisted, underwritten, and unwound before the collateral is.

Rank 1 therefore carries an obligation rather than a status. It means the engine has been
told that when it needs cash, your position is where cash comes from, and that the cash
arrives inside the window the unwind is scheduled against.

## The two routes, and how each of them fails

The routes are not two spellings of the same thing. They put the risk in different places
and they break under different conditions.

**Redemption is slow but contractual.** The position is redeemed with you and the proceeds
retire the debt. The counterparty is you, the timing is what you committed to, and if it
fails there is a term to point at. What it costs is time — and a breached account with debt
accruing is short of exactly that.

**Sale is fast but conditional.** The receipt is swapped on a DEX and the proceeds retire
the debt. You are not asked and you are not a party; the instrument changes hands and your
register moves. What it costs is certainty — the route depends on a book that owes nobody
anything, and depth withdraws under stress. The conditions that impair a strategy are the
same conditions that thin the market for its receipt.

That asymmetry is the reason the underwriting in [6.3](./6.3-underwriting-and-risk-parameters)
asks about both and accepts neither as a substitute for the other. A strategy underwritten
on depth alone is underwritten on the route most likely to be gone when it is needed.

The sale route is available at all only because of a property worth stating plainly: the
receipt is a transferable instrument. It is minted into the margin account and the client
never holds it, but the enforcement property there is possession, not transferability. A
transfer restriction that would block a sale of the receipt does not make the position safer.
It removes one of the two routes out of it.

## If your strategy relies on the redemption route

This is the position you are in when the receipt has no market that could clear inside the
window: the SLA is carrying the whole of rank 1.

### The SLA is a risk-engine input, not a marketing figure

In a fund fact sheet, "weekly liquidity" is a description of normal-course behaviour with
the manager's discretion sitting quietly behind it. Here it is a parameter the engine relies
on while executing a forced unwind under stress, on an account whose health is already below
1, with debt accruing.

Three differences follow.

**It is tested only in the bad state.** Nobody exercises your SLA at the top of the market.
The requests that matter are the ones generated by liquidations, which cluster, arrive
without warning, and correlate with whatever caused the impairment.

**It is not a request from the client.** The client does not initiate it and cannot delay
it. Your usual mitigations for redemption pressure — talking to the investor, negotiating
timing, offering in-kind — do not exist, because the counterparty on the other end is a
schedule.

**It is arithmetic, not service quality.** The SLA is a term in a solvency calculation. A
redemption that settles late is not poor service. It is an unhedged exposure for as long as
it is outstanding, on an account that has already breached.

### What happens if you gate or delay during a liquidation

Take an account in liquidation whose rank 1 position does not return cash on schedule —
because you gated, suspended, extended a notice period, settled in kind, or simply took
longer than stated.

**To the client.** The engine still has to restore health, and the position it was told to
unwind is not producing proceeds. It continues walking ranks. What it reaches is the
client's Bitcoin. The client's collateral is sold to cover a debt that a working rank 1
unwind would have covered — and it is sold at whatever price the market offers at that
moment, because rule-based enforcement determines that closeout begins, not the price at
which it completes. Their Bitcoin exposure ends, in a market that may have had nothing to do
with their loss. That is the precise outcome the rank ordering exists to prevent.

**To the position that remains.** A gated position is still marked, still carries a haircut,
and still sits in an account with debt accruing against it. It is not neutral to be stuck;
it is a live exposure that continues to consume the client's health.

**To the loan book.** Arch Prime does not fund client financing — third-party liquidity
providers do, and Arch Prime earns a spread on flow it does not finance. A shortfall that
survives the unwind is a loss against the book those providers fund. Those are the same
providers whose committed depth gates how much leverage the system can extend at all.
Committed lending dollars, not code, gate the launch. A strategy that impairs recovery does
not only harm the clients holding it; it reprices the credit that every other client draws
on.

**To you.** Expect a strategy that gates during a liquidation to lose rank 1, and expect
that to be functionally equivalent to losing access to levered flow.

### The two open questions on this route

Both of these are named open items. Neither has been decided, and a manager should not plan
around an assumed answer.

> **[OPEN — whether the redemption SLA is a contractual commitment with stated remedies, or a
> stated expectation the risk engine relies on informally, has not been decided; and whether
> any part of it is enforceable on-chain rather than only in contract is unresolved · owner:
> Product + Counsel]**

> **[OPEN — whether a manager's own stake in the strategy can be posted as a seizable
> backstop against redemption failure — and if so, in what form, held where, and seizable
> under what conditions — is an open design question with no decided answer · owner: Risk +
> Counsel]**

The second one is worth a sentence of context, because managers ask about it. Arch Network's
enforcement property on the Bitcoin leg is that seizure rights are capped, automatic and
dependent on nobody's cooperation. A manager backstop would be an attempt to extend something
like that property to the strategy leg. Whether that is achievable — legally, or in the
transaction shape — has not been settled. Where a strategy is relying on the redemption
route, the SLA is carrying the weight alone until it is.

## If your strategy has a liquid market in its receipt

This is a materially different position to be in, and it is worth being exact about what it
changes and what it does not.

**What it changes.** Long redemption terms stop being automatically disqualifying. A
strategy whose redemption is measured in weeks can still hold rank 1 if the receipt clears
inside the window. The forced flow may never arrive at your subscription desk at all,
because the position was sold rather than presented to you.

**What it does not change.** You cannot commit to depth. Redemption is something you owe;
depth is a condition of a market neither you nor Arch Prime controls, and it is observed
rather than promised. It can be present in every observation and absent on the day of the
cascade, and nothing in the registry entry obliges anyone to keep it there.

**What it does to your NAV.** A sale realizes market price, not NAV. The account is marked
at your NAV; the receipt fetches what the book will pay. That gap is not your strategy
impairing — you can be exactly where your NAV says you are and the sale can still print
below it — and under a cascade every account holding the same position is selling into the
same book. The print is public, and it will be read as a mark on your strategy whether or
not it is one.

**What you are still on the hook for.** The NAV that the account is marked at, the
disclosures in [6.5](./6.5-reporting-and-disclosure-obligations), and prompt notice of
anything that would impair either route — including a change in the receipt's transfer
mechanics, which can remove the sale route the same way a gate removes the redemption route.

## The window itself is not published

The one number this page needs — how long the liquidation window is — is not published, and
that gap now bites on both routes rather than one.

> **[OPEN — the length of the liquidation window a position must be unwound inside is not
> specified anywhere in the corpus; managers cannot self-assess rank 1 eligibility by either
> route without it · owner: Risk]**

Neither is the mechanism of the sale.

> **[OPEN — the mechanics of closeout by sale are unspecified: where the receipt is sold, how
> execution is routed, what constrains price or slippage, and what happens when the sale
> cannot clear the size required. No venue, routing logic or fee treatment exists to describe
> · owner: Product / Risk / Protocol Engineering]**

## What you supply, what Arch Prime supplies, what does not exist yet

**You supply:** a redemption window you can meet in the bad state; disclosure of every
mechanism in your governing documents that can suspend, gate, extend or alter settlement,
including ones you have never used; the transfer mechanics of the receipt and anything that
would restrict its sale; what you know about the market for the receipt, including how it
behaved the last time conditions were disorderly; the settlement asset and the operational
path by which cash reaches the account; and prompt notification when any of that changes.

**Arch Prime supplies:** the liquidation engine, the rank assignment, the health arithmetic
that determines when an unwind is demanded, and disclosure to the client of what your terms
are before they deposit.

**Arch Network supplies:** the authorization and settlement of the Bitcoin leg when the walk
reaches rank 99 because the rank 1 position did not produce proceeds by either route.

**Not yet available:** the liquidation window figure, the mechanics of the sale route, which
route is primary and who selects it, the contractual and on-chain status of the SLA, and any
backstop mechanism. All of them are flagged above.

## What does not hold

- Rank 1 is not permanent. It follows the terms and the market, and both change.
- Depth is not a commitment. A market that has cleared size before is evidence, not a term,
  and it is evidence about a state of the world rather than about you.
- A larger haircut is not a substitute for a working route out. A haircut changes what the
  position is worth; it does not change whether the money arrives in time.
- Meeting the SLA is not a claim about price. It is a claim about timing. Arch Prime makes no
  representation to clients about the price your redemption settles at, and none about the
  price a sale of the receipt prints at.
- Nothing here overrides your governing documents. If your documents permit a gate, the gate
  exists, and Arch Prime's assessment has to assume it can be used.
