This page is written for the party supplying the dollars: what you are underwriting when
you fund an Arch Prime account, what protects your claim, and what does not.

## Who supplies the credit

Arch Prime does not fund client financing. The dollars a Prime client borrows are supplied
by third-party liquidity providers. Arch Prime operates the account, sets and publishes the
risk parameters, and earns a spread on flow it does not finance. The closeout it relies on
runs as programs on Arch Network.

No lender, borrower or counterparty is named in these documents, and no pipeline size or
indicated commitment appears in them. That is a standing rule, not a gap in this draft.

## What you are underwriting

You supply your capital to Arch Prime. What you are underwriting is the enforcement Arch
Network provides, not the asset.

Those are two different parties and the distinction is the whole of your credit analysis.
Arch Prime is the business that opens the account, prices the counterparty and sets the
parameters your claim is computed against. Arch Network is the chain that holds the
collateral, authorizes any movement of it, and settles to Bitcoin with no discretionary
operator in the path. Your capital is exposed to the first. Your recovery depends on the
second.

Funding a Bitcoin-collateralised loan has historically meant taking possession of the
Bitcoin and then forming a view on two things: the price of the coin and the character of
the borrower. Here you form a view on a third thing instead — whether your claim executes
when the account breaches.

The client retains custody. The collateral remains an unspent transaction output on
Bitcoin, locked under predefined Taproot spending conditions, and is never reissued on
another chain. Enforcement runs on Arch Network and does not require possession: the seizure
rights are capped, automatic, and dependent on nobody's cooperation.

The boundary on that, stated in the same breath: enforcement determines that closeout
begins and in what order. It does not determine the price at which closeout completes.
Rule-based enforcement gives you certainty about the process and its timing, and no
certainty at all about the market you are recovering into.

## What protects the claim

**Rule-based closeout.** The account derives one health factor across all positions and
debts: `collateralValue = Σ (quantity × price × (1 − haircut))` and
`healthFactor = collateralValue / totalDebt`. Closeout is triggered when the health factor
falls below 1. The trigger is a computation on published parameters, not a credit
committee's decision, and it does not wait on the client's cooperation or on Arch Prime's
solvency.

**The borrower never holds the money.** Borrowing and deployment execute as one atomic
bundle: the system validates the collateral and the requested borrow, issues the debt,
deposits the proceeds into the whitelisted strategy, mints the receipt directly into the
margin account, registers it as a position, recomputes health and asserts it — or the whole
bundle reverts. There is no step at which the borrower has possession of the proceeds or of
the receipt. The receipt is a transferable instrument — closeout by sale depends on that —
but it is minted into the margin account and the client never holds it. The enforcement
property is possession, not transferability. That is enforcement by transaction shape, not
by policy, and there is no exit path that extracts borrowed capital from the account.

**Liquidation ordering touches Bitcoin last.** Closeout walks positions by liquidation rank
ascending until health is restored. Deployed strategy positions are rank 1. Bitcoin is rank
99. The levered leg — the position that actually caused the breach — is unwound before the
collateral you are lending against is touched. The test for rank 1 is unwindability inside
the liquidation window, by either route: the receipt is sold, or the position is redeemed
with the strategy. A position with a deep secondary market can qualify despite long
redemption terms; a position with neither a workable redemption window nor executable depth
cannot qualify at all.

**A published per-account borrow cap.** Rationing is by per-account borrow cap expressed as
a share of available depth. The recommendation is to start at 2% and step down as depth
grows. The consequence for a lender is arithmetic rather than promissory: a 2% cap
admits 50 accounts at full cap, so no single account can be a majority of what
you funded.

**Native Bitcoin collateral, and a dollar leg that cannot manufacture credit.** The
collateral is the coin itself, not a claim on a coin held somewhere else. On the dollar
side, archUSD is a 1:1 stablecoin-backed dollar with permissionless mint, custodied at
BitGo. It is not a CDP. It wraps dollars that already arrived; it does not manufacture
lending capacity. Nothing in the stack creates its own credit and then presents it to you
as a settlement asset.

## What does not protect the claim

**Market tail risk.** Certainty about when closeout begins is not certainty about the price
it completes at. A fast enough drawdown, a thin enough book, or a gap through the
liquidation band can leave recovery short of the debt. No parameter on this page bounds
that.

**The gap between NAV and what the receipt sells for.** Where closeout is executed as a
sale of the receipt rather than a redemption with the strategy, what is realized is a market
price, not NAV. The account is marked at NAV; a receipt sold into the market fetches what
the book will pay. Under a cascade, liquidations sell into the same book, and the gap widens
exactly when your recovery depends on it not widening. This is a distinct loss channel. It
is not the strategy impairing — the strategy can be exactly where its NAV says it is and the
sale can still come up short — and it stacks on top of the impairment risk below.

Whether closeout runs as a sale or as a redemption is itself unsettled, so a lender cannot
yet size which of the two failure shapes their recovery is exposed to.

> **[OPEN — which unwind route is primary (sale of the receipt or redemption with the
> strategy), who selects it at closeout, and whether it varies by strategy, are not settled.
> Both routes are described here; neither is presented as the normal path · owner: Product /
> Risk]**

**Strategy NAV impairment.** In a levered account the binding constraint is not Bitcoin's
price — it is the net asset value of the deployed position. For an account with equity `E`
and deployed position `V`, the impairment that wipes equity is `E / V`. As illustrative
arithmetic, not a product parameter: 1 BTC at $100,000 as collateral, $500,000 deployed and
$500,000 of debt means a 20% strategy impairment wipes the client at full Bitcoin value,
while a 100% Bitcoin drawdown leaves the account whole. The position you are exposed to
through the borrower is the deployed one.

V1 runs one whitelisted strategy, which means 100% concentration by construction. That is
acceptable only because the strategy is hand-underwritten, and it is not a precedent.
Per-strategy and per-manager caps arrive in phase two. The strategies listed are
market-neutral in construction, which bounds directional exposure and bounds nothing else:
they carry execution, model, counterparty, venue and operational risk, and their yield can
go to zero or negative.

**Utilisation at the moment you want out.** Target utilisation is 85%, and the rate curve
inflects at 90%; 85% is chosen to preserve withdrawal headroom. Headroom is not a
guarantee. If utilisation is at or above target when you ask to withdraw, your capital is
lent out and your exit depends on borrowers repaying or being closed out, not on a
committed liquidity line.

**The validator assumption.** The principal risk in Arch Network is the validator
honest-majority assumption. If participants controlling more than the signing threshold
collude, they can authorize transactions the rules do not permit. This is not one risk among
several. Upgrade authority over Arch Network's programs is a real central point of failure
and is disclosed as one. Note where this one sits: it is not a risk Arch Prime's own conduct
bounds, and it is the reason your recovery depends on a party other than the one you
contracted with.
