title: Reading account state

This page tells you what state a Prime account exposes to a reader, and the two things
about that state — settlement stage and freshness — that a product displaying it has to
get right.

The account state is Arch Prime's. The settlement stage underneath it is Arch Network's,
and the first correctness point below is exactly the seam between them.

## What there is to read

Four layers, in the order a product usually needs them.

**Account-level health.** The single derived number: `healthFactor = collateralValue /
totalDebt`, where `collateralValue = Σ (quantity × price × (1 − haircut))`. Liquidation is
triggered when `healthFactor < 1`. This is the number the system acts on and the number
your user will look at first.

**Per-position contribution to margin.** Each position's `quantity × price × (1 − haircut)`
is its contribution to collateral value. Showing the aggregate without the breakdown hides
the thing that actually moves: in a levered account the binding constraint is not
Bitcoin's price, it is the deployed position's NAV. A user who cannot see which position
is carrying the account cannot act on the number you gave them.

**The liquidation ordering.** Each position's liquidation rank, ascending. Deployed
strategy positions are rank 1; Bitcoin is rank 99; Bitcoin is the last thing touched. This
is what lets you answer "what would I lose first," which is a different question from "how
close am I."

**The collateral registry parameters.** Per eligible asset: valuation source, haircut, max
account weight, liquidation rank, borrow eligibility, redemption terms. These are the
inputs to everything above, and they change by Arch Prime's governance and underwriting
rather than by market movement, so they are the layer you can cache longest — but not
indefinitely.

## The correctness point: which state are you showing?

Agreement on Arch Network application state does not by itself move BTC. Authorization of
the Bitcoin transaction is a separate step. Conflating the two is the most common misreading
of the architecture, and a UI that reports one while implying the other is the most likely
way a builder passes that misreading on to a user.

And the second step has its own qualifier: pre-confirmation is not Bitcoin finality. A
transaction can leave the mempool before confirmation, and a Bitcoin reorganization can
alter recently confirmed state.

So there are at least three distinct things a product might mean by "your withdrawal went
through":

| What happened | What it means | What it does not mean |
|---|---|---|
| Arch Network state agreement | The bundle or instruction was applied to Arch Network application state | That any Bitcoin has moved |
| Bitcoin transaction authorized and broadcast | The Bitcoin leg exists and is in flight | That it is confirmed, or that it will be |
| Confirmed on Bitcoin, to some depth | It is in the chain at that depth | That it is beyond reorganization at shallow depth |

A builder displaying a position must be explicit about which of these a given value
reflects. Two rules that follow from that:

- **Label the settlement stage on the value, not in a footnote.** A balance that reflects
  Arch Network state and a balance whose Bitcoin leg has confirmed are different numbers with
  different failure modes, and the difference is not cosmetic.
- **Do not present a pre-confirmation state as final.** Name the specific assumption you
  are asking the user to accept, and name the depth at which you stop asking.

> **[OPEN — the confirmation depth at which a Bitcoin settlement is treated as final for
> display and for accounting purposes is not stated in this set · owner: Protocol
> Engineering / Risk]**

## The freshness point: an arithmetically correct wrong number

The health factor is a pure function of its inputs. That is a strength for auditability
and a hazard for display, because the arithmetic cannot tell you that a price is an hour
old or that a strategy NAV has not printed since yesterday.

A stale price or a stale NAV produces a health factor that is arithmetically correct and
materially wrong. The account may already be liquidatable at true values while the number
you are showing sits comfortably above 1, or the reverse.

This is sharper for deployed positions than for Bitcoin. A price feed updates on market
cadence; a strategy NAV updates on the strategy's cadence, which is slower and less
uniform. Since the deployed leg is the term that dominates a levered account's health, the
slowest-updating input is usually the one carrying the most weight.

**Surface staleness rather than smoothing it.** Concretely:

- Show the age of the valuation behind any health figure you display, next to the figure.
- Do not interpolate, extrapolate, or carry forward a NAV to make a chart continuous. A
  flat line that is honest about not having updated is better than a smooth line that
  invents movement.
- Treat "no fresh valuation" as its own display state, not as "unchanged."
- If you compute health yourself from component reads, the result is only as fresh as your
  oldest input, and you should show that age rather than the newest one.

> **[OPEN — valuation freshness metadata available to a reader is not specified: whether
> account and position state carry a valuation timestamp, a source identifier, and a
> staleness indicator · owner: Protocol Engineering / Risk]**

## Where the read interface is defined

Not here. This page describes what state exists and what it means. The callable
surface — how you request it, what comes back, how it is authenticated, what is paginated,
what is streamed and what errors look like — belongs to the Arch developer book, and this
set does not restate it.

> **[OPEN — the callable read interface for Prime account, position and registry state is
> not specified in this set: request and response shapes, authentication, freshness
> semantics, and any event or subscription delivery · owner: Developer Relations /
> Protocol Engineering]**

## What you supply, what Arch Prime and Arch Network supply, what is not yet available

**You supply:** the reads, your own caching policy, the timestamp and settlement-stage
labelling on everything you render, and a defined behaviour for the stale-input case.

**Arch Prime supplies:** the account, position, debt and registry state; the derived health
factor; and the liquidation ordering.

**Arch Network supplies:** the application state that agreement is reached on, and
settlement on Bitcoin as an ordinary Taproot-signed transaction with no distinguishing
marker.

**Not yet available:** a specified freshness contract, a stated confirmation-depth policy,
and a published liquidation threshold — so a warning tier you build now is your threshold,
not Arch Prime's.

> **[OPEN — liquidation threshold / LLTV is not published; any warning band a builder
> displays is the builder's own choice and must be labelled as such · owner: Risk]**

## What does not hold

- **A read is not a lock.** State observed at read time can change before your user's
  action is applied, and health is recomputed and asserted inside the bundle regardless of
  what you displayed.
- **Health is not netted.** V1 margin is deliberately additive, with no correlation
  modelling. Do not present offsetting exposure that the system does not compute.
- **The registry is not static.** Haircuts, ranks, weights and eligibility are risk
  parameters. Cache them, but re-read them, and never hard-code one into a client.
- **Bitcoin's price is not the headline.** For a levered account it is close to the least
  informative number you can put at the top of a screen.
